## Product overview <a href="#product-overview" id="product-overview"></a>

The Unitree A2 is a 12-joint legged platform with two battery bays, a platform computer and a separate Intel Core i7 development computer. A2 Pro adds sensing and expansion options; it is an option within this legged guide. The wheeled A2-W has its own chassis envelope, mass, motors and control requirements and is documented separately. The manufacturer's optional wheel-leg replacement statement does not make the delivered configurations interchangeable in software. [A2 comparison](https://www.unitree.com/A2), [User Manual V1.7](https://marketing.unitree.com/article/en/A2/User_Manual.html).

Use the robot label, purchase option, battery labels, sensor part numbers and installed image together to identify a unit. Record the app version, robot firmware, user-computer operating system and SDK commit before development. Neither a store SKU nor the repository's A2 example establishes a tested firmware pairing. This guide reaches an original offline state-processing result; live commissioning remains conditional on the delivered configuration.

## Applications <a href="#applications" id="applications"></a>

A2 can support supervised perception studies, payload integration and inspection-route development within its documented load, environment and terrain limits. A perception payload changes mass, center of gravity, clearance and endurance; the static standing load is not its moving payload allowance. Autonomous navigation and following require a separate application design, sensor calibration, localization and an operator-controlled environment. Camera/LiDAR presence alone does not prove a navigation stack or safety certification. [User Manual, overview and precautions](https://marketing.unitree.com/article/en/A2/User_Manual.html).

Begin development with inventory and offline state handling, then an edition- and firmware-approved subscriber. Mapping, learning, simulation, low-level motor control, charging-dock automation and field deployment are later projects. No matching BSP (board support package), wheel/leg firmware matrix, simulator configuration or deployed learning policy has been verified for the delivered A2. Do not fill those gaps using Go2 settings or family names.

### Development and deployment boundaries <a href="#development-and-deployment-boundaries" id="development-and-deployment-boundaries"></a>

Treat state observation, media capture, map editing and movement as separate capabilities with separate ownership. A research navigation example or a working offline parser does not establish an industrial autonomous mission, certified stop, payload retention or environmental clearance. Optional computers, radios, cameras and docks require their exact model/edition and power/network budgets. The first result remains the original offline program below.

## Hardware and connections <a href="#hardware-and-connections" id="hardware-and-connections"></a>

### A2 power, computers and expansion ports <a href="#a2-power-computers-and-expansion-ports" id="a2-power-computers-and-expansion-ports"></a>

The side-interface figure distinguishes PC1 and PC2 USB/Ethernet paths, leg power/RS485 connectors, and expansion power with CAN/RS485. Its first table identifies PC2 USB 2.0 Type-C with PD limited to 20 W, PC2 USB 3.0 limited to 1 A, and PC1 USB 3.0 ports individually limited to 2 A. Circular Ethernet connectors carry Ethernet **and 12 V** on a manufacturer-specific 8+2-pin connector. They are not ordinary RJ45 sockets or evidence of standards-compliant PoE. Use the correct supplied/approved breakout, not a cable inferred from the drawing. [User Manual, Interface Description, source page 10](https://marketing.unitree.com/article/en/A2/User_Manual.html).

The same figure labels 24 V/CAN, battery-voltage/CAN, 12 V/RS485 and 24 V/RS485 outputs. The lower table and drawing disagree on callouts 13–15, including a leg label; a table row is not a reliable cavity assignment. Published 24 V 5 A / 12 V 6 A entries do not resolve that numbering conflict or the permissible aggregate load. Keep custom harness fabrication, CAN/RS485 termination and external power loading blocked until Unitree supplies the exact connector drawing, mating view, wire map and revision-specific current budget. Leg connectors marked 58 V are not auxiliary low-voltage logic ports. [Original interface figure](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/static/2026/9/16/a10ace30f9a942149c700edde568ed66_2481x3509.png).

Both slots use the model's 50.4 V nominal, 9 Ah / 453.6 Wh battery packs; maximum charger output is 58.8 V. The 58/58.8 V maximum region is different from nominal 50.4 V. The charger manual specifies 58.8 V, 7 A (411.6 W), AC 100–240 V, 50/60 Hz, and about 1 h 50 min charging under stated conditions. Do not substitute an As2 50.4 V charger because a connector appears similar. [Battery/charger manual V2.0](https://marketing.unitree.com/article/en/A2/Battery_Charger.html).

The user manual describes dual-battery status colors and one-button startup: blue can represent one discharging pack and the other standby; green can represent one pack or dual discharge when voltages are close. A color alone does not prove redundancy or a successful hot swap. The range page advertises hot swapping, but battery handling instructions say to switch packs off for insertion/removal. A revision-specific live-swap procedure is unresolved; initial integration uses powered-down inspection and no live battery removal. [User Manual, startup](https://marketing.unitree.com/article/en/A2/User_Manual.html), [battery handling](https://marketing.unitree.com/article/en/A2/Battery_Charger.html), [range features](https://www.unitree.com/A2).

### Charging, storage and the pre-power inspection <a href="#charging-storage-and-the-pre-power-inspection" id="charging-storage-and-the-pre-power-inspection"></a>

Use the matched charger on a clear, hard surface with ventilation and supervision. The manuals call for the pack off and removed from the robot for cable charging, AC connected before the pack, and a hot pack allowed to cool to room temperature. Prefer the manual's 5–40 °C charging environment; the BMS 0–50 °C protection window is not a recommended charging setpoint. The BMS provides cell balancing, overcharge/overdischarge and short-circuit protection, charging-temperature/current checks, pack load detection and fault indication. Load detection can prevent a removed pack from switching on and can switch it off after removal; that is not a hot-swap qualification. Protection/precharge behavior is internal to the matched charger/pack system, not a user recovery procedure. The 1 Hz charging indication and all indicators off at completion apply to the documented charging state, not every possible off/fault state. Check the model-specific LED matrix before diagnosing a fault. [Charging chapter](https://marketing.unitree.com/article/en/A2/User_Manual.html), [battery protections, indicators and precautions](https://marketing.unitree.com/article/en/A2/Battery_Charger.html).

Battery guidance recommends storage around 20–25 °C and periodic charge checks; for prolonged storage, below 30% recharge to about 70% as specified. The battery book's recommended discharge environment of −5 to 55 °C is narrower than the robot table's −20 °C lower bound. Confirm the assembled system's battery/temperature approval rather than promising full performance at the robot's headline minimum. Swelling, leakage, damaged shells, wet contacts or abnormal heat require removal from service and manufacturer support, not a BMS bypass or a recovery-charging experiment. Dock/contact-charger descriptions are not permission to connect an unverified dock. [Battery manual](https://marketing.unitree.com/article/en/A2/Battery_Charger.html).

Before any later authorized operation, inspect foot pads, joint assemblies and fasteners, camera/LiDAR windows, fan intake/exhaust, dry battery contacts and positive latch engagement. Check remote charge, centered sticks and non-sticking buttons. Unpack and repack using this model's leg-folding figures and designated case recesses. Keep hands away from hip/knee pinch points and use the designated lifting locations and handling personnel. The placement figure shows belly supports level on a flat surface with folded lower legs and feet supported, with no limb trapped beneath the chassis. It is a preparation requirement, not permission to power the robot during the offline exercise. [Preparation and packing chapters](https://marketing.unitree.com/article/en/A2/User_Manual.html), [placement figure](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/static/2026/9/16/36c09bc92aa6402191dfcec28387506a_2481x3508.png).

### Portal hardware revision, rails and isolation <a href="#portal-hardware-revision-rails-and-isolation" id="portal-hardware-revision-rails-and-isolation"></a>

The A2/A2W portal distinguishes legged and wheeled products and expressly says they cannot be converted into each other by exchanging legs. A2 has 12 motors; A2W adds four wheel actuators for 16. The A2W article describes190×51 mm tires,24 N·m maximum wheel torque and 11 rad/s at 58 V, distinct from walking speed. Its “replaceable wheel-legs” row does not override the no-conversion note. The general product joint ranges and the later joint-coordinate table differ; use the delivered model's mechanical drawing and calibrated coordinate convention rather than combining maxima. [About A2](https://support.unitree.com/home/en/A2_SDK_Development_Guide/about_a2); [About A2W](https://support.unitree.com/home/en/A2_SDK_Development_Guide/about_a2w).

On the numbered chassis interface table, USB ports connect to different computers: PC2 has a DP-capable Type-C and a separate USB port limited to 1 A; PC1's two USB ports each list2 A. Switch2 is 192.168.124.0/24, Switch1 is 192.168.123.0/24. The 24 V ports share a6 A module, and 12 V outputs share another6 A module. Port 10 is described as24 V5 A in About A2, but Custom Peripherals calls it6 A; the total 6 A shared rail does not resolve that per-port discrepancy. VBAT output is described as58 V/10 A total, not regulated24 V. Match the exact port label/revision and approved harness before connection; do not use leg-power connectors as generic accessory outlets merely because a broad paragraph says “any2+2”. [numbered chassis and rail tables](https://support.unitree.com/home/en/A2_SDK_Development_Guide/about_a2); [Custom Peripherals](https://support.unitree.com/home/en/A2_SDK_Development_Guide/custom_peripherals).

The cable tables number power−/485 A/485B/power+ as1/2/3/4 for the depicted2+2 cable; the 8+2 cable has power− at 1,12 V+ at 10, and Ethernet pairs2/3=4N/4P,4/5=1P/1N,6/7=3N/3P,8/9=2N/2P. Two extra dark-blue/yellow wires are unassigned. Preserve the source connector view and keyed alignment; no mating-side mirroring is implied. The A2 emergency-stop table identifies pins 2 and 4 as E-Stop and says connecting them triggers it. This is an interface description, not a rated safety circuit: obtain the actual button/harness and verified stop category before use. [cable and emergency-stop definitions](https://support.unitree.com/home/en/A2_SDK_Development_Guide/about_a2).

A2's optional NX docking station has two enclosure versions: V1 external antennas and V2 internal antennas/raised cover, with the source saying identical compute/interfaces but different top clearance. Its 36–60 V input, pass-through2 A output,24 V3 A per-port outputs with 5 A shared limit,12 V4.5 A network outputs with 5 A shared limit, USB power limits and reset-versus-hiccup behavior are module-specific. They are not the chassis6 A rail. The 1000M/2.5G network ports and PTP option likewise belong to that dock revision. GNSS must sit beside the antenna box, not on top, to avoid the documented interference. Install drawings use M6x14 hardware on the chassis rail, but final fastener/clearance approval remains specific to the delivered kit and payload. [docking station tables](https://support.unitree.com/home/en/A2_SDK_Development_Guide/about_a2); [Install guide for docking and modules](https://support.unitree.com/home/en/A2_SDK_Development_Guide/docking_installation).

The A2 dock manual places its user computer at 192.168.123.164, provides display/SSH/headless NoMachine paths, maps USB-CAN/485 device nodes, and documents4G, raw GNSS/gpsd, backup and restore. Those are deployment/maintenance functions, not prerequisites to the offline result. Device-node names can enumerate differently; confirm the physical mapping. Backups need actual restore verification and home-directory scope; a snapshot listing is not a complete image/recovery guarantee. ### What the pinned SDK2 source actually supports <a href="#what-the-pinned-sdk2-source-actually-supports" id="what-the-pinned-sdk2-source-actually-supports"></a>

[Pinned A2 state example](https://github.com/unitreerobotics/unitree_sdk2/blob/b7fec6020eb93d4c059a6edf19905e12a75a8d3d/example/a2/sport/a2_sport_state.cpp), [channel subscriber](https://github.com/unitreerobotics/unitree_sdk2/blob/b7fec6020eb93d4c059a6edf19905e12a75a8d3d/include/unitree/robot/channel/channel_subscriber.hpp).

The GO `SportModeState_` contract contains `stamp`, `position[3]`, `velocity[3]`, `mode`, `progress`, IMU, gait and foot/path fields. `stamp` is a `TimeSpec_` containing signed 32-bit `sec` and unsigned 32-bit `nanosec`; `mode` is uint8. Keep source timestamps separate from a local monotonic receipt clock; do not compute wall-clock latency by assuming an epoch. Display mode numerically: the upstream example indexes a fixed name array without an explicit bounds check. An unfamiliar numeric mode must not crash a reader or be relabeled as an assured control state. [GO state contract](https://github.com/unitreerobotics/unitree_sdk2/blob/b7fec6020eb93d4c059a6edf19905e12a75a8d3d/include/unitree/idl/go2/SportModeState_.hpp), [time contract](https://github.com/unitreerobotics/unitree_sdk2/blob/b7fec6020eb93d4c059a6edf19905e12a75a8d3d/include/unitree/idl/go2/TimeSpec_.hpp).

### Ownership: observation, RPC and low-level commands <a href="#ownership-observation-rpc-and-low-level-commands" id="ownership-observation-rpc-and-low-level-commands"></a>

A channel subscriber observes data; an RPC client invokes services, and a command publisher can drive actuators. A factory domain or an API version is not a firmware release number. The model manuals explicitly distinguish high-level and low-level development: in high-level mode remote and API commands can both be processed; in low-level mode remote commands are disabled. Assign one command owner across app, R3, companion follow, voice actions, autonomous services and development processes. A successful subscriber does not obtain ownership, stop another controller or make the area motion-free. [User Manual, developer-mode discussion](https://marketing.unitree.com/article/en/A2/User_Manual.html).

Do not add motion-switcher `ReleaseMode`, stand, move, damping, wheel commands or a low-level writer to a state check. Release/ownership operations change controller state and need a separate model/firmware-specific procedure. Demonstration binaries may actuate immediately after startup or enter indefinite loops; review each source before eventual supervised commissioning. The state example itself loops forever and its sleep/unused `dt` member does not document a publishing rate. Low-level remote disablement means an R3 button cannot be assumed to remain an effective stop path in custom control. [Pinned state example](https://github.com/unitreerobotics/unitree_sdk2/blob/b7fec6020eb93d4c059a6edf19905e12a75a8d3d/example/a2/sport/a2_sport_state.cpp), [manual control modes](https://marketing.unitree.com/article/en/A2/User_Manual.html).

SDK2 root code is BSD-3-Clause; third-party middleware/binaries retain their own notices. Keep the pinned license and dependency notices when redistributing SDK-derived code. Manufacturer manuals retain their copyright; link the originals rather than republishing whole OCR transcripts. The checkpoint here is an original standard-library exercise, with no vendor code copied into its implementation. [SDK2 license](https://github.com/unitreerobotics/unitree_sdk2/blob/b7fec6020eb93d4c059a6edf19905e12a75a8d3d/LICENSE).

### Portal descriptions and the pinned implementation <a href="#portal-descriptions-and-the-pinned-implementation" id="portal-descriptions-and-the-pinned-implementation"></a>

The source audit uses C++ SDK2 `b7fec6020eb93d4c059a6edf19905e12a75a8d3d`, not a moving branch. At this pin, initialize the factory with domain and network-interface arguments only: `Init(domain, interface)`. The A2/As2 portal signature includes a third shared-memory argument, and the B2 article mentions it in prose despite its two-argument signature. That additional argument is not in the pinned header. Likewise, the portal's `GetServerApiVersion` reference return differs from the actual by-value `std::string`; `GetApiVersion` is a separate client-side version query. Do not construct a compile recipe by mixing those revisions. [pinned factory](https://github.com/unitreerobotics/unitree_sdk2/blob/b7fec6020eb93d4c059a6edf19905e12a75a8d3d/include/unitree/robot/channel/channel_factory.hpp); [pinned client](https://github.com/unitreerobotics/unitree_sdk2/blob/b7fec6020eb93d4c059a6edf19905e12a75a8d3d/include/unitree/robot/client/client.hpp).

The subscriber paragraph mistakenly mentions a send channel. The real subscriber creates a receive channel, associates a callback and can use a bounded queue; copy required values before callback storage is reused. A positive queue length can absorb work but cannot make stale data fresh or prevent overload indefinitely. `GetLastDataAvailableTime` describes local receipt timing, not device sensor time. Domain numbers, NIC selection and multicast configuration are transport choices, not authentication or isolation. [pinned subscriber](https://github.com/unitreerobotics/unitree_sdk2/blob/b7fec6020eb93d4c059a6edf19905e12a75a8d3d/include/unitree/robot/channel/channel_subscriber.hpp).

A successful RPC return only reports that request's result. It does not certify safe posture, fresh state or exclusive motor ownership. The documented common errors distinguish client-side request/API-registration/timeout/correlation/response-validation/lease failures (3102–3107) from server-side internal/unsupported/parameter/rejection/lease failures (3202–3207). Specifically, 3103 means the API is not registered locally, 3106 means invalid response data, and 3203 separately means the server has not implemented the API. Keep the service name, API version, request identity and error together. Do not repeatedly replay a timed-out movement request: the robot may have acted before the reply was lost. Publisher, mode release, service switch, calibration, docking and navigation requests remain active operations. No such operation is part of the first check in this page.

Put those devices on 124 with an independently approved routing policy. A domain ID or different topic is not physical isolation. The PC2 AP-route scripts make persistent network changes and are not simple passive readers; Windows route-helper availability does not make the robot SDK Windows-supported. [User Development Unit](https://support.unitree.com/home/en/A2_SDK_Development_Guide/develop_module); [Custom Peripherals](https://support.unitree.com/home/en/A2_SDK_Development_Guide/custom_peripherals); [Debugging Specification](https://support.unitree.com/home/en/A2_SDK_Development_Guide/debugging_specification).

Preserve `/home/unitree/slam_config`, `/home/unitree/dist` and `/home/unitree/graph_pid_ws`; the source discourages upgrading/removing existing system packages and suggests isolated application dependencies. It does not supply a universal recovery image. ## Getting started <a href="#getting-started" id="getting-started"></a>

### First result: offline sport-state metadata <a href="#first-result-offline-sport-state-metadata" id="first-result-offline-sport-state-metadata"></a>

Prerequisites: an ordinary computer with Python 3.8 or later and this complete code. No robot, battery connection, SDK package, network interface, DDS discovery or ROS installation is required. The synthetic dictionary resembles selected GO sport-state fields; it is **not** a DDS decoder or a A2 firmware test. For a later live adapter, the edition and exact firmware contract must first be approved.

1. Record the intended model/edition and unresolved image/BSP in your project notes. Leave the robot powered down and disconnected for this exercise.
2. Save the following complete original code as `offline_state.py`. It copies timestamp/vectors into a frozen snapshot and accepts unfamiliar modes as numbers.
3. Run `python3 offline_state.py` locally. No installation command is needed.
4. Compare the two output lines below. A single message, repeated timestamp or stale local receipt must remain a waiting condition. The two-second freshness limit is an example application policy, not a Unitree timing guarantee.

```python
"""Original offline metadata exercise. No SDK, network, device or robot access."""
from dataclasses import dataclass
from math import isfinite

@dataclass(frozen=True)
class Snapshot:
    stamp: tuple
    position: tuple
    velocity: tuple
    mode: int
    received: float

def capture(message, received):
    sec, ns = message['stamp']
    if type(sec) is not int or type(ns) is not int:
        raise ValueError('integer timestamp fields required')
    if not -(2**31) <= sec < 2**31 or not 0 <= ns < 1_000_000_000:
        raise ValueError('timestamp out of range')
    vectors = [tuple(float(x) for x in message[k])
               for k in ('position', 'velocity')]
    if any(len(v) != 3 or not all(isfinite(x) for x in v) for v in vectors):
        raise ValueError('finite three-element vectors required')
    mode = message['mode']
    if type(mode) is not int or not 0 <= mode <= 255:
        raise ValueError('uint8 mode required')
    if not isfinite(received):
        raise ValueError('finite local receipt time required')
    return Snapshot((sec, ns), *vectors, mode, float(received))

def assess(samples, now):
    if len(samples) < 2:
        return 'WAIT: two messages required'
    first, last = samples[0], samples[-1]
    if not isfinite(now) or not 0 <= now - last.received <= 2.0:
        return 'WAIT: latest local receipt is stale'
    if last.received <= first.received or last.stamp <= first.stamp:
        return 'WAIT: timestamp must advance'
    return 'OFFLINE PASS: advancing stamp and fresh copied metadata'

if __name__ == '__main__':
    message = dict(stamp=[12, 100], position=[0, 0, 0],
                   velocity=[0, 0, 0], mode=255)
    first = capture(message, 10.0)
    message['stamp'][1] = 200
    last = capture(message, 10.1)
    print(assess([first, last], 10.2))
    print('mode=%d position=%s' % (last.mode, last.position))
```

**Expected result:**

```
OFFLINE PASS: advancing stamp and fresh copied metadata
mode=255 position=(0.0, 0.0, 0.0)
```

This pass demonstrates copied metadata, a timestamp transition and a fresh synthetic local receipt. It says nothing about physical motion, robot health, coordinates, sensor accuracy, network transport or command ownership. The program does not interpret mode 255 as a known state. If no transition is observed, keep the diagnostic in WAIT rather than calling one received message a working stream.

Its physical Emergency Stop button is not the R3 software damping combination. Follow its G/neutral/S mapping and original drawings; do not transfer a plain-R3 key diagram. Remote stickers, App import/update and pairing are revision-specific resources. Store it dry, avoid hot sun and match battery/storage conditions. [Intergrated Remote Controller](https://support.unitree.com/home/en/A2_SDK_Development_Guide/intergrated_remote).

For a fault, keep raw mainboard/motor/BMS words and LED pattern with the exact source table. Mainboard bits include upper/lower/IMU/motor/BMS/remote timeouts, model mismatch, soft-start, emergency-stop, USB, joint-limit and secondary-battery faults. Motor and battery alarms use different namespaces; mV/mA and pack/cell quantities must remain distinct. An all-zero default record is not evidence that alarms are absent. No generalized fault-clear, calibration, firmware or restart sequence is supplied here. [Common Mistakes and Definitions](https://support.unitree.com/home/en/A2_SDK_Development_Guide/common_mistakes_and_definitions); [Basic Service Interface](https://support.unitree.com/home/en/A2_SDK_Development_Guide/basic_service_interface).
