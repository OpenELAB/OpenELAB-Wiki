# Unitree A1 Standard and Exploration: legacy SDK and manual guide

{% hint style="info" %}
**Manufacturer reference: Unitree A1 Standard and Exploration.** An exact OpenELAB robot URL/SKU has not been verified. The historical manual's “A1 PRO” running header is not evidence of another retail edition. This guide gives a read-only source-inspection first result; physical setup remains conditional on the delivered hardware, image and remote.
{% endhint %}

Unitree A1 is a 12-actuator quadruped with depth perception, high-level body control and low-level joint access. The official Standard/Exploration distinction concerns perception and navigation equipment. Its observed legacy SDK support is **v3.3.1**, not the Go1-only v3.8.6 branch. [Official A1 features and variants](https://www.unitree.com/A1), [pinned v3.3.1 support statement](https://github.com/unitreerobotics/unitree_legged_sdk/blob/20929b54030c0d8ca4e7ac49ec2a02c447cb9fcd/README.md)

## Product overview <a href="#product-overview" id="product-overview"></a>

Standard includes a depth camera, video transmission, human recognition/following and real-time high/low APIs. Exploration adds the listed LiDAR mapping, localization, planning and dynamic obstacle-avoidance functions and describes an AI compute module for vision work. Voice intercom, six-microphone array, wide-angle camera and ultrasound are optional modules, not a universal installed sensor set. Neither a generic SDK nor the word “Exploration” identifies the exact onboard image or sensor calibration.

The available OEM manual has a **V1.1, 2020-03 cover**, despite a distributor filename ending in v1.0. Its running header says A1 PRO and its PDF metadata contains Laikago text. The actual A1 cover and content control this guide's scope; those inherited labels cannot establish a Laikago manual or an A1 Pro sales SKU. [Manual cover](https://www.mybotshop.de/Datasheet/Unitree_A1_User_Manual_v1.0.pdf#page=1)

## Applications <a href="#applications" id="applications"></a>

* **Control education and exported-state analysis:** separate joint position/velocity/torque data from whole-body control, while preserving actual controller units and motor order.
* **Depth and human-following projects:** use the documented depth range and confidence display as perception information, not a guarantee that a route is safe.
* **Exploration navigation research:** LiDAR and the corresponding software are needed for the listed mapping and planning tasks; Standard hardware does not acquire those capabilities through a model-name setting.
* **Model and algorithm development:** the official simulation catalog includes an A1 model and joint controllers. Simulation-controller availability does not establish a supported physical bridge. [A1 product sections](https://www.unitree.com/A1), [official model catalog](https://github.com/unitreerobotics/unitree_ros/blob/157ff950351e5f535e03c5d85a16b7d594b3dbcc/README.md)

## Hardware and connections <a href="#hardware-and-connections" id="hardware-and-connections"></a>

The [source-page-49 interface figure](https://www.mybotshop.de/Datasheet/Unitree_A1_User_Manual_v1.0.pdf#page=49) shows a TX2 HDMI/USB group, two Ethernet positions, two 24 V input positions, regulated 5/12/19 V outputs and a mini-PC USB/HDMI group. Its numbered map labels outputs **5 V 2 A, 12 V 2 A and 19 V 2 A**. This is the illustrated 2020 configuration, not proof that all Standard or Exploration units include TX2 or the same connectors. No verified connector polarity, pin assignment, acceptable external-input current or cable selection is provided here. External power and charging remain blocked until the matching electrical instructions are available.

The battery introduction describes **4200 mAh, 21.6 V**. It does not establish a charger by multiplying or rounding those values. The manual discusses overcurrent/temperature protection, removed-pack charging, cooling, LED status, storage and transport; battery-use text at 5–40°C differs from the later whole-robot/storage conditions around 5–35°C. Use neither as an inferred extension of the other. [Battery chapters, pp.13–17,44–46](https://www.mybotshop.de/Datasheet/Unitree_A1_User_Manual_v1.0.pdf#page=13)

Startup in the old manual can automatically raise the robot. Its stop chapter describes a remote OFF action cutting power, with fall risk, binding/range conditions and a protective frame. Its remote chapter says low-level development makes remote commands ineffective and high-level commands can conflict. Therefore no generic A1 button combination, remote-stop guarantee or unsupported stationary hold is prescribed. Confirm the delivered mode, image, remote, mechanical support and effective independent stop before any live session. [Startup, pp.12–13](https://www.mybotshop.de/Datasheet/Unitree_A1_User_Manual_v1.0.pdf#page=12), [remote, pp.20–23](https://www.mybotshop.de/Datasheet/Unitree_A1_User_Manual_v1.0.pdf#page=20), [emergency handling, pp.39–40](https://www.mybotshop.de/Datasheet/Unitree_A1_User_Manual_v1.0.pdf#page=39)

The manual's motion charts contain context and labeling inconsistencies, including mismatched shoulder-button references. Its mode-specific SLAM/climbing chapter and general terrain section also state different angle limits. These are source-specific descriptions, not a sequence to combine. Foot-pressure air paths, left/right pads and wear must be serviced against the exact diagram, not guessed from a force reading. Whole-robot use is described as dry and non-dusty with clearance requirements that differ between introductory and later safety text. [Motion charts, pp.24–32](https://www.mybotshop.de/Datasheet/Unitree_A1_User_Manual_v1.0.pdf#page=24), [conditions, pp.41–43](https://www.mybotshop.de/Datasheet/Unitree_A1_User_Manual_v1.0.pdf#page=41)

## Getting started <a href="#getting-started" id="getting-started"></a>

### First result: qualify a proposed A1 source baseline <a href="#first-result-qualify-a-proposed-a1-source-baseline" id="first-result-qualify-a-proposed-a1-source-baseline"></a>

Prerequisites: a browser, no robot connection, and the exact files linked below. The result is a documented compatibility decision, not live telemetry.

1. Open the [v3.3.1 README](https://github.com/unitreerobotics/unitree_legged_sdk/blob/20929b54030c0d8ca4e7ac49ec2a02c447cb9fcd/README.md). Locate the A1 sport-mode condition and explicit Go1/Laikago exclusions. Record the complete pinned commit and `A1 sport_mode ≥1.19`.
2. Inspect [`comm.h`](https://github.com/unitreerobotics/unitree_legged_sdk/blob/20929b54030c0d8ca4e7ac49ec2a02c447cb9fcd/include/unitree_legged_sdk/comm.h): find `HIGHLEVEL`, `LowState`, `tick` and `HighCmd`. Record `0x00`, µs, 20 motor slots and the actual field spelling. Do not open a UDP socket.
3. Inspect [`a1_const.h`](https://github.com/unitreerobotics/unitree_legged_sdk/blob/20929b54030c0d8ca4e7ac49ec2a02c447cb9fcd/include/unitree_legged_sdk/a1_const.h) and [`quadruped.h`](https://github.com/unitreerobotics/unitree_legged_sdk/blob/20929b54030c0d8ca4e7ac49ec2a02c447cb9fcd/include/unitree_legged_sdk/quadruped.h). Distinguish model-specific constants from joint ordering. A 20-value motor array does not make A1 a 20-joint robot.
4. Inspect [`example_position.cpp`](https://github.com/unitreerobotics/unitree_legged_sdk/blob/20929b54030c0d8ca4e7ac49ec2a02c447cb9fcd/examples/example_position.cpp). Locate the safety/model selection and timed joint commands. Record that its default AlienGo target prevents treating it as a ready A1 diagnostic.
5. Compare the [Go1-only v3.8.6 README](https://github.com/unitreerobotics/unitree_legged_sdk/blob/4539a6c10dfbc9781cea6fcb7d51bc6ddc6f71e1/README.md). If a proposed installation uses that branch for A1, classify it as unsupported by the observed README and stop before installation or connection.

**Expected result:** a record containing `A1 / SDK v3.3.1 / sport_mode ≥1.19 required / HIGHLEVEL 0x00 / tick µs / AlienGo example default requires review / hardware not tested`. If source values differ, verify the commit in the address bar before drawing a compatibility conclusion. If the robot's installed sport-mode version cannot be established, the missing prerequisite remains open.

For an already exported state log, first verify decoder/revision and units. Require an actual first sample, a subsequent advancing tick and a current local receive time; reject stale, repeated, backward, nonfinite or uninitialized records before interpreting joint or contact data. A 2000 µs interval equals 2 ms; treating it as 2000 ms would invalidate timing analysis. This arithmetic is an interpretation example, not a measurement of an A1.

## Troubleshooting and FAQs <a href="#troubleshooting-and-faqs" id="troubleshooting-and-faqs"></a>

**Why does the SDK identify a different model?** Check the exact release, constructor/safety selection and actual robot revision before touching network settings. A1 appears alongside AlienGo in v3.3.1; examples do not all select A1 automatically.

**Why are timestamps or motion rates unrealistic?** First check the µs tick against any Go1-derived ms conversion, then leg/joint ordering and receive freshness. Do not repair the symptom by changing control frequency on hardware.

**Why is SLAM or LiDAR absent?** Inspect the actual Exploration sensor/compute configuration and image. The Standard feature list does not promise the LiDAR navigation stack. The 3D app model is a display, not confirmation of live localization.

**Does a tracking box or foot-end water resistance make outdoor operation safe?** No. Confidence changes indicate tracking quality, while the manual restricts the whole robot's environment. Inspect sensors, light, route and mode against the matching operating instructions; avoid turning a perception feature into a safety claim.

**Can the old manual's button chart be used directly?** Only after exact controller/image qualification. Resolve chart/text differences, high/low control conflict and power-cut fall behavior with the delivered instructions. This guide's first result intentionally needs no button action.
