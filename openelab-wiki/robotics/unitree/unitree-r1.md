## Product overview <a href="#product-overview" id="product-overview"></a>

Unitree R1 is a 1.23 m humanoid platform whose edition changes both its joint configuration and its development access. **R1 AIR has 20 joints, R1 has 26, and R1 EDU supports configuration-dependent 26–40 joints. Only EDU is marked for secondary development in the official comparison.** A published SDK repository or an educational application does not turn the base R1 into an EDU unit. This guide covers identification, charging, supported handling, operator setup, recovery, and a conditional read-only EDU telemetry check. ## Applications <a href="#applications" id="applications"></a>

R1 and AIR support supervised operation and interaction through the manufacturer application/controller. EDU additionally provides the development route for sensor integration, state acquisition, simulation transfer and control research. Start a laboratory project by documenting edition, installed modules, software and controller behavior, then receive state before considering an actuator command. The source manuals do not establish unattended operation, industrial safeguarding, weather resistance, or a payload rating suitable for every arm posture. [P](https://www.unitree.com/R1) [U4](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/static/2026/7/8/8561d363d02b4795902af2e68e864f4e_2482x3509.png) [U30](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/static/2026/7/8/2403d80b393048c6be0bdbdf649e6a44_2481x3509.png) [U31](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/static/2026/7/8/64c2b104d080436a9b264e30540b014d_2481x3509.png)

The built-in microphone and camera make speech/vision interaction possible, but permission to capture data, accessible software interfaces and an installed optional compute module are separate questions. Do not turn a camera feature table into a working driver claim, or the EDU label into proof that an optional hand or Orin is installed. The BMS energy-reserve note does not prove an explanation for that arithmetic. OEM pack/charger pairing is the supported path; replacement supply design remains unresolved.

The manual's arm row is labeled torque but uses kilograms, and its 2 kg figure differs from the live product page's 3–5 kg wording. Neither is used here as a torque limit or guaranteed payload. Mechanical range diagrams likewise do not define safe command limits, collisions, or joint-controller settings. ## Hardware and connections <a href="#hardware-and-connections" id="hardware-and-connections"></a>

### Prepare the site and identify the support hardware <a href="#prepare-the-site-and-identify-the-support-hardware" id="prepare-the-site-and-identify-the-support-hardware"></a>

Use a dry, open, level area, keeping the robot in view with at least 2 m clearance from people, obstacles, water and uneven terrain. The robot operating range stated in the manual is 0–40 °C. It is neither waterproof nor dustproof; avoid rain, wet floors, dust/gravel, ice, soft foam and slippery surfaces. Strong radio interference and obstacles can weaken controller links. Battery-only temperature limits do not extend the complete robot's operating range. [U11](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/static/2026/7/8/666ac993e3b04f17adada270a6b2baa3_2482x3509.png) [U12](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/static/2026/7/8/4f7e5626c16f4371ba2fa34b34eea459_2481x3509.png)

Unpack on level ground, with the case facing upward. Inventory the robot, matching battery/charger, controller, stand and supplied accessories. Before power, check shell damage, joints, foot pads, battery socket/keying, latches, fans, neutral sticks and free-moving controller buttons. Keep hands away from joint gaps. Use a two-person handling plan for case transfers; the lightest edition still weighs about 27 kg. [U12](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/static/2026/7/8/4f7e5626c16f4371ba2fa34b34eea459_2481x3509.png) [U15](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/static/2026/7/8/96c09f9ec9ac4c1b83cb01d3ee756cb0_2482x3509.png) [U27](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/static/2026/7/8/64863003dcb64aceb7a3b3553cb4a5d9_2482x3509.png) [U28](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/static/2026/7/8/2b9f7d43c05640de900bbaa2cc954070_2482x3509.png) [U34](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/static/2026/7/8/31e78977a3264c029a5d1bf9c2f8d7c1_2482x3509.png)

The stand comprises a base, support sleeve/inner tube, wave washer, quick-release clamp, protective foam and top bracket. Assemble the base/rod/washer, orient the clamp as drawn, extend the inner tube, fit foam and bracket, then secure height and clamping force. **The AIR/base and EDU hanging orientations differ.** Verify the actual engagement before loading it. No torque value is specified in the reviewed instructions; do not invent one or infer a stand load rating. See Unitree's [assembly figures](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/static/2026/7/8/b67d0e5f47d0468ba7507a6be111a1eb_2481x3509.png), [clamp figure](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/static/2026/7/8/30926ce98e2e4c53925b018993adb2e7_2481x3509.png) and [edition-specific hanging diagram](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/static/2026/7/8/96c09f9ec9ac4c1b83cb01d3ee756cb0_2482x3509.png).

The manual gives M4 mounting-hole information and a front-panel modification procedure, but that is not a free external mounting interface. Use the original [mounting drawing](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/static/2026/7/8/4edc89d4669e42169248cc0cd2437b04_2481x3509.png) and obtain the exact revision's accessory instructions before drilling, removing panels, adding mass or rerouting wires. Backpack, hand and radar installation are referred to videos, not fully specified in the accessible pages. No connector pinout, auxiliary power budget or verified CAD package was established here. [U31](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/static/2026/7/8/64c2b104d080436a9b264e30540b014d_2481x3509.png)

### Charge the robot battery outside the robot <a href="#charge-the-robot-battery-outside-the-robot" id="charge-the-robot-battery-outside-the-robot"></a>

1. Secure the robot in the documented unpowered support position. Switch the pack off before inserting or removing it; remove it from the robot for charging. Do not treat automatic unplug protection as permission to hot-swap. Check the casing, plug and cable; stop for damage, swelling, moisture or impact history. [B3](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/static/2026/5/27/fb6f99e718964d9f9dc900ac12250b12_2481x3508.png) [B4](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/static/2026/5/27/189a5575e3df42c18da4810e7764a3a6_2481x3508.png) [B8](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/static/2026/5/27/f3bb0079409f4022b72ee76b11907036_2481x3508.png)
2. Let a warm pack cool to room temperature. The dedicated battery manual recommends a 5–40 °C charging environment; keep the pack and charger visible on a noncombustible surface with ventilation and no flammables nearby. Its protective cutoff temperatures are not operating targets. [B4](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/static/2026/5/27/189a5575e3df42c18da4810e7764a3a6_2481x3508.png) [B9](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/static/2026/5/27/fe3d698052d34fa5a1e2b84b4be20e78_2481x3508.png)
3. Verify the official R1 charger nameplate against the supply, connect AC first, then connect the switched-off battery using the keyed connector. Use the original [charging connection diagram](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/static/2026/5/27/1080117f60e64adfb52e9d22f4c1ab43_2482x3508.png); it is not a pinout for making an adapter. [U9](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/static/2026/7/8/399496826ed548d095d94591b27d732f_2481x3509.png) [U10](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/static/2026/7/8/5e026cde02c44322807aea2287ea2781_2482x3509.png) [B6](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/static/2026/5/27/1080117f60e64adfb52e9d22f4c1ab43_2482x3508.png)
4. Watch the charging indication. The pack's LEDs progress through the indicated charge bands, and all pack indicators off is the manual's fully-charged state. Cross-check the charger's status; darkness alone also requires checking power, connections and faults. Disconnect after charging. The remote uses a different completion indication: all its charging LEDs on. [B7](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/static/2026/5/27/7b063b3180cb40848242e0aedb618fa3_2481x3508.png) [R6](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/static/2026/5/26/583308f05ac548dc8fff085d17f8ebe7_2482x3508.png)

Do not charge inside the robot, while the pack is on, immediately after a hot run, or with an unofficial charger. Do not open, bridge, probe with conductive tools, crush or puncture the pack. Do not retry a protected pack before identifying and resolving the cause. [B6](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/static/2026/5/27/1080117f60e64adfb52e9d22f4c1ab43_2482x3508.png) [B8](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/static/2026/5/27/f3bb0079409f4022b72ee76b11907036_2481x3508.png) [B9](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/static/2026/5/27/fe3d698052d34fa5a1e2b84b4be20e78_2481x3508.png)

### Charge and inspect the controller separately <a href="#charge-and-inspect-the-controller-separately" id="charge-and-inspect-the-controller-separately"></a>

With the controller off, connect its Type-C port to the recommended 5 V / 2 A USB supply. Its charge indication flashes at about 1 Hz and becomes fully lit at completion; unplug then. Fit the removable joystick tips clockwise without forcing them. The remote's advertised >100 m range is for an open environment, not a working safety separation or a guarantee through walls. [R3](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/static/2026/5/26/f9d61c8a5f3042f7b4420527bdc6b135_2481x3508.png) [R6](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/static/2026/5/26/583308f05ac548dc8fff085d17f8ebe7_2482x3508.png)

The new remote has a dedicated stop button and a separate stop-link indicator. A blinking stop indicator means that stop unit is not connected to the robot; a steady light indicates link readiness. Do not equate the main DL link with this separate stop-link check. The stop indicator turns red on activation and green on deactivation; its button has a three-second retrigger lockout. Verify the physically supplied controller, because the manual warns that old and new versions may differ. [R2](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/static/2026/5/26/811c3ea381ec4d0ab788169e9ec184e6_2481x3508.png) [R5](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/static/2026/5/26/3e198574d90f448591658be9e31b9739_2481x3508.png) [U25](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/static/2026/7/8/96b7ca834f134233aac66e0e7396ceef_2481x3509.png)

## Software and development resources <a href="#software-and-development-resources" id="software-and-development-resources"></a>

### Edition access, software pairing and ownership <a href="#edition-access-software-pairing-and-ownership" id="edition-access-software-pairing-and-ownership"></a>

Use [Unitree Explore for R1](https://www.unitree.com/app/R1/) for the operator workflow described in the manual. Record the app build, robot firmware, battery/controller versions when available, serial number, edition, joint count and installed peripherals before an update. The manuals describe OTA but do not identify a validated firmware/SDK pair for the actual delivered U1–U6 packages. An unqualified “update everything” instruction would erase that compatibility record. [U15](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/static/2026/7/8/96c09f9ec9ac4c1b83cb01d3ee756cb0_2482x3509.png) [U20](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/static/2026/7/8/3965ae3ae0c247e69d1fb9c13c7dfa2e_2481x3509.png) [U23](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/static/2026/7/8/13dcba915c7b4df6b833703f2fc95fa2_2481x3509.png)

The official C++ SDK2 snapshot reviewed here is `b7fec6020eb93d4c059a6edf19905e12a75a8d3d`; its README lists Ubuntu 20.04 LTS, aarch64/x86\_64 and GCC 9.4.0, with CMake ≥3.10. These are the SDK's stated host prerequisites, not proof of the onboard R1 image. Python SDK2 snapshot `814556d15970dd2ecf1c9984e845ca02ab07e206` lists Python ≥3.8 and CycloneDDS 0.10.2, plus NumPy and OpenCV. No package was installed, built or run for this guide. See the pinned [C++ README](https://github.com/unitreerobotics/unitree_sdk2/blob/b7fec6020eb93d4c059a6edf19905e12a75a8d3d/README.md) and [Python README](https://github.com/unitreerobotics/unitree_sdk2_python/blob/814556d15970dd2ecf1c9984e845ca02ab07e206/README.md).

The inspected [R1 Python example](https://github.com/unitreerobotics/unitree_sdk2_python/blob/814556d15970dd2ecf1c9984e845ca02ab07e206/example/r1/low_level/r1_low_level_example.py) uses `unitree_hg.msg.dds_.LowState_` on `rt/lowstate`. It describes a **26-actuator** R1 layout whose positions in the 35-slot HG message array are:

```
Left leg:   0..5       Right leg: 6..11
Waist:     12,13       Left arm: 15..19
Right arm: 22..26      Head:     29,30
```

This is an example-specific map, not 35 physical motors and not a contiguous 0–25 HG array. It does not cover AIR, 40-joint EDU, dexterous hands or R1-A5/A7. The [HG schema](https://github.com/unitreerobotics/unitree_sdk2_python/blob/814556d15970dd2ecf1c9984e845ca02ab07e206/unitree_sdk2py/idl/unitree_hg/msg/dds_/_LowState_.py) confirms `tick`, `mode_machine`, `imu_state`, a 35-element `motor_state`, remote bytes and CRC; it does **not** include a battery state-of-charge field. Do not label a motor's `vol` field as pack voltage or invent an HG battery percentage.

**Keep command ownership explicit.** The user manual says the built-in motion program starts automatically and periodically sends zero-speed commands. High-level remote/API commands may coexist and destabilize the robot; low-level development can make ordinary remote commands ineffective. The manual's EDU debug procedure requires suspension/damping and includes a position-mode posture change. It is not a read-only diagnostic. [U4](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/static/2026/7/8/8561d363d02b4795902af2e68e864f4e_2482x3509.png) [U30](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/static/2026/7/8/2403d80b393048c6be0bdbdf649e6a44_2481x3509.png) [U31](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/static/2026/7/8/64c2b104d080436a9b264e30540b014d_2481x3509.png)

The inspected R1 example calls `ReleaseMode()`, creates an `rt/lowcmd` publisher and writes joint commands; do not run it just to see status. A read-only first check must neither release the current controller nor enter debug/position mode, publish motor commands, start a locomotion client or call an actuator example. Clearing controller ownership is a separate commissioning step that needs the exact firmware procedure and an established support/stop plan. ## Getting started <a href="#getting-started" id="getting-started"></a>

### First result for every edition: identify and connect without a motion command <a href="#first-result-for-every-edition-identify-and-connect-without-a-motion-command" id="first-result-for-every-edition-identify-and-connect-without-a-motion-command"></a>

This checkpoint begins with a robot already positioned and powered by a trained operator under the correct manual procedure. If the robot is uncommissioned, first read the supported startup choices below. This guide's first result does not request standing, walking, calibration, emergency-stop activation or a debug transition.

1. Record the invoice SKU, robot edition/serial and physical joint configuration. For EDU U1–U6, record actual hand, compute and sensor modules rather than assigning them from the U-code. Check battery/controller charge and the fitted remote's stop-link readiness. ### Supported startup choices: do not mix their state transitions <a href="#supported-startup-choices-do-not-mix-their-state-transitions" id="supported-startup-choices-do-not-mix-their-state-transitions"></a>

The following explains the manufacturer workflows for planning a supervised commissioning session. They are not steps to perform as part of the status-only checkpoint.

| Path            | Required preparation and sequence                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           | State boundary                                                                                                                                 |
| --------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Supine startup  | Lay the unpowered robot flat, face up, limbs natural, in the cleared area; insert the switched-off pack and verify both latches. Apply the model-matched power sequence and wait for the startup indication. The manual then uses locked standing followed by the lie-to-stand command, and only after stable standing enters walking/running.              | Lock mode is not proof of balance. `L2+X` changes body posture; `R2+A` enables walking/running. Keep these out of a read-only check.           |
| Bracket startup | Assemble/engage the correct edition's bracket, hang the robot facing forward with limbs clear, install the pack and power under the matching procedure. The manual then enters standing lock, has the operator support the waist from the front, lifts off the bracket, places both feet on the floor, and enters walking/running before releasing support. | The manual explicitly says it cannot independently balance during that transfer. Do not release it in lock mode or improvise powered handling. |

See [supine figures, scans 15–17](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/static/2026/7/8/905048d9d6e64228a0b81315b2e7bea4_2482x3509.png) and [bracket figures, scans 17–20](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/static/2026/7/8/b490b9e0ef454df680796937fbecfa3c_2481x3509.png). The remote table resolves locked-standing to `L2 + D-pad up` (short press); lie/stand is `L2+X` held about 2 seconds. These gestures are motion/state transitions, not universal reset shortcuts. [R9](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/static/2026/5/26/f8c91663b6b04e4bac4e320dfc8069aa_2481x3508.png)

**Power-button timing conflict:** robot manual scans 16/18/27 say short press then hold >2 seconds; the dedicated BT2-72 manual scan 6 says short press then hold >3 seconds. Match the installed pack/revision and resolve discrepancies with Unitree; do not repeatedly cycle power to guess. The remote's short-press then >2-second hold belongs to the remote, not the pack. [U16](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/static/2026/7/8/905048d9d6e64228a0b81315b2e7bea4_2482x3509.png) [U18](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/static/2026/7/8/3327b6de3a504a8681c085804d08070e_2481x3509.png) [U27](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/static/2026/7/8/64863003dcb64aceb7a3b3553cb4a5d9_2482x3509.png) [B6](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/static/2026/5/27/1080117f60e64adfb52e9d22f4c1ab43_2482x3508.png) [R4](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/static/2026/5/26/2d50842fe43a4ebbb219057176c5a434_2481x3508.png)

### Conditional R1 EDU 26-actuator telemetry checkpoint <a href="#conditional-r1-edu-26-actuator-telemetry-checkpoint" id="conditional-r1-edu-26-actuator-telemetry-checkpoint"></a>

Proceed only on an identified, already commissioned **R1 EDU with the exact 26-actuator configuration**, manufacturer-confirmed firmware/IDL pairing, and an already provisioned host/network. The inaccessible developer portal prevents this guide from verifying the R1-specific IP/subnet, interface allocation, onboarding procedure or firmware pairing. Do not reuse a Go2 IP, install packages speculatively or change the active robot's network just to run this check.

For a later authorized session on that verified setup, the original OpenELAB `r1_read_state.py` accompanying this guide subscribes to `rt/lowstate`, reads for at most ten seconds and closes the subscriber after receiving three distinct ticks or timing out. It imports only `ChannelFactoryInitialize`, `ChannelSubscriber` and the HG `LowState_` from SDK2. Its receive calls and timeout/close behavior follow the inspected [channel implementation](https://github.com/unitreerobotics/unitree_sdk2_python/blob/814556d15970dd2ecf1c9984e845ca02ab07e206/unitree_sdk2py/core/channel.py). It never creates a robot command publisher or requests control ownership.

```bash
python3 r1_read_state.py --interface YOUR_VERIFIED_ROBOT_INTERFACE --edition R1-EDU-26
```

`YOUR_VERIFIED_ROBOT_INTERFACE` is a required substitution supplied from the approved setup record. The `--edition` option is an operator acknowledgment, not automatic hardware detection. The script checks a 35-slot message before extracting the 26 positions above. It displays raw joint `q` values, `tick` and `mode_machine`; do not infer units, calibration quality or safe ranges from plausible numbers.

**Expected result:** three records with distinct ticks, followed by a telemetry-only PASS message. No numeric log is claimed as observed here. On no data, wrong array length, type errors or unexpected edition, close the reader and inspect firmware/interface/IDL support; do not call `ReleaseMode()`, start a publisher, disable control services or enter debug to “fix” reception. A subscriber still participates in DDS discovery; it is not electrically passive packet capture. The script and SDK have not been executed against a robot.

### Stop and recovery are different operations <a href="#stop-and-recovery-are-different-operations" id="stop-and-recovery-are-different-operations"></a>

| Condition / control                            | Documented effect                                        | Recovery boundary                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| ---------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Soft stop: `L2+B` held >5 s in walking/running | Enters damping; the robot can descend/fall to the ground | Clear the fall area; do not treat it as an upright hold. Inspect and re-establish the documented support/state before restarting normal operation.                                                    |
| Dedicated hard-stop button, new remote         | Removes joint power; the robot can fall immediately      | Keep clear. The manuals require a robot power restart and at least three seconds before restarting after activation. Green on the remote only means its stop request is released, not robot recovery. |
| Double-click START during dance                | Pauses the dance movement                                | It is a dance-specific pause, not the hard-stop circuit or a general low-level SDK stop.                                                                                                              |
| Remote stop indicator blinking                 | Stop unit is not linked                                  | Stop setup; establish the correct stop-unit connection before operation. Steady DL alone is insufficient.                                                                                             |

### Read the chest and battery indicators in context <a href="#read-the-chest-and-battery-indicators-in-context" id="read-the-chest-and-battery-indicators-in-context"></a>

The user manual maps cyan breathing to boot, purple to zero torque, orange to damping, white to running, dark blue to standby, red to malfunction, yellow to debug and green to seated. Blue breathing indicates audio recording; green breathing indicates a voice command received after wake-up. A purple light after the startup voice is not evidence that the robot can balance. [U29](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/static/2026/7/8/72542788ebdc432a8be735658e4d8fa9_2481x3509.png)

The BT2-72 manual distinguishes **off/outside robot**, **off/inserted**, **discharging**, **charging** and **fault** indications. Do not read all states using one generic four-LED percentage table. A short press while off queries charge. Its inserted/off table prints an anomalous “25%–29%” band where the neighboring table has “25%–49%”; this is not silently corrected here. Use the actual operating state, charger status and source figure, and obtain clarification if that band affects a decision. [B5](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/static/2026/5/27/1db7ed44838044c3a8a6ef375e1df3c3_2482x3508.png)

For discharging, four solid green LEDs correspond to 88–100%; alternating full/partial steps use a flashing LED as the level falls. The final flashing red/green state represents 0–4%. The dedicated battery manual advises ending robot use below 40% and changing/charging the pack; do not plan to wait for final-LED warning or the 25.2 V BMS cutoff. A cutoff can remove support power and cause a fall. [B4](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/static/2026/5/27/189a5575e3df42c18da4810e7764a3a6_2481x3508.png) [B5](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/static/2026/5/27/1db7ed44838044c3a8a6ef375e1df3c3_2482x3508.png) [B8](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/static/2026/5/27/f3bb0079409f4022b72ee76b11907036_2481x3508.png)

**Battery fault interpretation:** LED1 red blinking/solid is labeled pack damage; LED2 red blinking/solid low/high temperature; LED3 red blinking/solid over/under-voltage; LED4 red blinking/solid discharge/charge overcurrent. All-red breathing/blinking patterns indicate a short circuit. LEDs 1/4 blinking with 2/3 solid is identified as a communication fault needing both pack and robot returned for service. The manual separately says an LED3 charging error points to the charger, so record whether the fault occurred during charge or discharge rather than diagnosing solely by LED number. See the original [fault table and LED orientation](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/static/2026/5/27/7b063b3180cb40848242e0aedb618fa3_2481x3508.png). Reuse is allowed only after the cause is resolved; do not bypass protection or try to energize a damaged pack. [B8](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/static/2026/5/27/f3bb0079409f4022b72ee76b11907036_2481x3508.png)

### Shutdown, storage and maintenance <a href="#shutdown-storage-and-maintenance" id="shutdown-storage-and-maintenance"></a>

Normal shutdown is a controlled physical transition: establish the supported clear area, use the documented lie-down sequence, switch the pack off with the revision-matched procedure, confirm off, then remove it. The fallback in the manual for a failed remote requires the robot to be lying flat before battery-button shutdown; power loss while standing can drop it. This is not permission to approach active joints or manually wrestle an unstable robot. [U26](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/static/2026/7/8/3f556c83df5640d4a11dc553c6fddc32_2481x3509.png) [U27](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/static/2026/7/8/64863003dcb64aceb7a3b3553cb4a5d9_2482x3509.png) [U32](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/static/2026/7/8/29065e0de1794b0bb9f75b570c881993_2481x3509.png)

For packing, use the original fitted case, two-person lift and the [orientation/padding instructions](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/static/2026/7/8/64863003dcb64aceb7a3b3553cb4a5d9_2482x3509.png): back/head down, arms aligned, chest liner in place, legs fitted into their liner recesses, all contact surfaces buffered and accessories secured. Do not remove a live pack to make the robot fit. [U28](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/static/2026/7/8/2b9f7d43c05640de900bbaa2cc954070_2482x3509.png)

Clean only when powered off, using a soft dry cloth; keep camera surfaces, fans, microphone/speaker openings and foot pads unobstructed. Inspect screws, latches, cables, pads and shell before/after sessions. The ankle maintenance figure identifies A/C fill points and B contact areas, but specifies no lubricant grade or service interval; obtain those before servicing rather than inventing a grease. Damaged parts need official service. [U33](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/static/2026/7/8/f61c85278f924639816d1783fb206c37_2482x3509.png) [U34](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/static/2026/7/8/31e78977a3264c029a5d1bf9c2f8d7c1_2482x3509.png) [U35](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/static/2026/7/8/cc2c70c4183846f69bacacca7b364f1f_2481x3509.png)

Store the removed pack dry, away from heat, conductive objects and children. The dedicated manual gives 22–28 °C as ideal storage and asks for periodic checks: if below 30%, recharge to 70% before continued storage. Never leave it empty for a long period. Its broad battery-use temperature range does not override the robot's 0–40 °C condition. Its transport paragraph's “about 65%” is a source statement, **not a carrier/shipping approval**; obtain current requirements from the supplier/carrier before shipment. Do not deliberately run an uncommissioned robot to discharge a pack for transport. [B8](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/static/2026/5/27/f3bb0079409f4022b72ee76b11907036_2481x3508.png) [B9](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/static/2026/5/27/fe3d698052d34fa5a1e2b84b4be20e78_2481x3508.png) [B10](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/static/2026/5/27/126be7f6717543ac91bcc52707493cb2_2481x3508.png)

Do not reuse or ship a swollen, punctured, wet or heavily impacted pack. Arrange qualified battery handling/recycling; do not attempt to force-discharge a damaged pack. Battery disposal, radio permissions, warranty and shipping depend on the actual product and location. The manuals' own legal, radio and warranty chapters are linked in the source index rather than reproduced as new OpenELAB terms.
