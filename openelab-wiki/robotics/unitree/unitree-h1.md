# Unitree H1: 19-joint hardware, power scope and state-only development

{% hint style="info" %}
[**Unitree H1 — verified OpenELAB product listing**](https://openelab.io/products/unitree-h1-humanoid-robot-with) · SKU `Unitree-H1` · observed catalog has one Default Title option (product `8846453113030`, variant `45731749626054`). This identifies the model, not a guaranteed hand, computer, battery or firmware bundle.
{% endhint %}

Unitree H1 is a full-size humanoid research platform. This guide covers its own hardware, operating manual and a conditional **state-only** SDK2 checkpoint. H1 has 19 active body joints and uses the GO DDS message family; humanoid form does not make it compatible with H1-2’s HG decoder. See the separate [H1-2 guide](unitree-h1-2.md) for the other robot.

## Product overview <a href="#product-overview" id="product-overview"></a>

H1 combines a torso computer, depth/laser sensing, articulated legs and arms, a waist joint and two removable battery packs. Unitree’s [current H1/H1-2 comparative product table](https://www.unitree.com/h1/) and [model-specific overview diagram](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/static/2025/4/18/efd7915fc35f4b4da79e6a81a4723931_2481x3509.png) describe the platform. H1’s single ankle degree of freedom per leg and four-joint arms distinguish it from H1-2’s two-axis ankles and seven-joint arms.

The audited catalog contains exactly one H1 SKU and no separate options for development entitlement, hands, compute, chargers or firmware. A supplier-confirmed serial-number/BOM and controller revision are prerequisites for selecting accessories and commissioning. The manuals describe development functions, but they do not certify that every retail unit exposes the same network/API/control permissions.

## Applications <a href="#applications" id="applications"></a>

Use the documented platform for supervised locomotion research, perception integration, state logging and, after a separate approved commissioning process, controller development. State logging is the smallest software result here: receiving one correctly decoded message makes model/interface errors visible before any custom command publisher exists.

The supplied controller manual describes motion, standing and certain H1 gestures; it explicitly excludes current stair operation. Optional hands and an expanded arm are separate configurations, not evidence of a verified manipulation bundle. Neither manual establishes unattended operation, person-carrying, collaborative safety certification or a safe proximity envelope around moving joints. Operators must be at least 18 and trained on the exact configuration. Keep line of sight, a prepared frame and the model’s operating clearances.

### Revision and interpretation boundaries <a href="#revision-and-interpretation-boundaries" id="revision-and-interpretation-boundaries"></a>

The manufacturer’s aggregate 864 Wh/maximum 67.2 V row and the accessory’s 432 Wh/33.6 V charging figure refer to different scopes. Two packs are shown in the robot, but the reviewed sources do not establish the electrical topology; do not infer a series/parallel harness or a 67.2 V pack charger.

The GO state array has 20 slots, while H1 has 19 active body joints. The pinned example defines `H1_NUM_MOTOR = 20` and `kNotUsedJoint = 9`; neither means 20 physical joints. Remote document version, Python package version, DDS runtime version and robot firmware version are separate identifiers.

Insert both **off** packs from the side, switches toward the robot’s front, and confirm each latch clicks and is seated. Correct orientation rather than forcing an incompletely seated pack. Manufacturer quick-swap language is not proof of hot-swap capability: power down and establish support before removal. ### Battery, charger and controller handling <a href="#battery-charger-and-controller-handling" id="battery-charger-and-controller-handling"></a>

The H1-only battery V1.0 [original rating table](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/static/2024/8/15/0cc0ecae04344a689a65527a1784ae20_2481x3508.png) specifies each pack at **28.8 V nominal, 15 Ah/432 Wh, charge limit 33.6 V**, size 120 × 80 × 182 mm. The listed charger is 154 × 60 × 36 mm, input 100–240 V AC, 50/60 Hz, 4 A/350 VA, output **33.6 V, 9 A, 302.4 W**, with about 1.5 h source charge time. Check actual matching nameplates; do not use the aggregate robot maximum voltage to select a charger.

The [pack power and charge diagram](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/static/2024/8/15/1877f49946684e7d8e821b60ccb3e6a3_2482x3508.png) gives a brief press followed by a hold longer than 2 s for normal on/off. The user manual calls for both pack buttons together on startup. A hold longer than 15 s is the accessory’s forced-cutoff operation, not routine startup troubleshooting. Remove and turn off packs for charging, connect the original charger to matching AC first, then the pack, after cooling. Supervise in a dry, ventilated noncombustible location; ideal charging is 5–40 °C, while the BMS charge-protection thresholds are below 0 °C or above 50 °C. Protection thresholds are not target operating temperatures.

The BMS document describes balancing, overcharge/short protection, charge interruption above 15 A, discharge cutoff at 24 V and idle self-discharge to 65% after ten days above 65%. These are internal protection descriptions, **not** supply/current settings or a reason to bypass shutdown. Stop use below the manual’s 40% recommendation. Its −15 to 55 °C pack-use range does not expand the robot’s 0–40 °C environment. Store removed packs dry/cool (22–28 °C recommended), avoid above 40 °C, and restore to 70% when below 30%. Damaged/wet/impacted packs require supplier service and appropriate recycling, not disassembly or improvised discharge. Manufacturer transport-state advice does not establish carrier/regulatory permission.

The exact-model remote manual covers **R3 and older R1**; identify the physical remote first. R3: 780 mAh, nominal about 5 h, listed 5 V/700 mA supply parameter, with off-state USB-C charging from 5 V/2 A; full-charge LEDs are **all on**. R1: 2500 mAh, about 4.5 h, 5 V/2 A, and full-charge LEDs **all off**. Both quote >100 m in an open environment, not a guaranteed indoor link. See [R3 parameters](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/static/2025/4/18/c3f46e3e080e4a89a53250c9e282ebed_2482x3509.png), [R3 charging/binding](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/static/2025/4/18/f019a909a90a4a80b2ee49ceda8b7bb0_2481x3509.png), [R1 parameters](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/static/2025/4/18/be8de50c52534ebfa0940f76272b7033_2481x3509.png) and [R1 charging](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/static/2025/4/18/292640a1b80c4be0beb981a22c9768cd_2481x3509.png). These accessory values are supported by this model’s own remote document.

For stick drift, the remote document uses F1+F3 while sticks are untouched, then full-range stick movement and F3 to save after beeping stops. Calibrate the remote with the robot safely supported and no active motion command path. R3 feedback is changed by triple F3 (sound/vibration) and triple F1 (on/off); holding F1 during shutdown saves the preference. An unconnected, idle R3 may turn off after ten minutes. Remote sound, link LEDs and battery LEDs describe different conditions.

## Getting started <a href="#getting-started" id="getting-started"></a>

### First result: one model-specific state message, conditionally <a href="#first-result-one-model-specific-state-message-conditionally" id="first-result-one-model-specific-state-message-conditionally"></a>

This workflow is for an already authorized development unit with an existing approved SDK2 host environment. It is not permission to install software, flash firmware, wire a custom harness or energize an unsupported robot. If the edition/firmware/network contract is not confirmed, stop after the physical/document identity checkpoint; the draft remains useful without claiming telemetry was obtained.

1. Record `Unitree-H1`, robot serial, exact pack/charger IDs, remote R1/R3 revision, installed computers/sensors/hands, firmware and current controller ownership. Confirm this is H1, not the other H1-family robot. Match the H1 pack and charger labels to the separate accessory document before charging.
2. Have a trained operator prepare the approved protective frame with secured base, both shoulder attachments and feet clear. Inspect connectors, latches, foot rubber, optics and fans, and establish the exact startup reference posture from the linked diagram. Use a dry 0–40 °C, flat/high-traction, observed work area with at least 2 m clearance. Clear personnel before energizing.
3. With two correctly fitted off packs, the own-model startup procedure is a brief press of both power buttons together followed by holding both for more than 2 s. Allow about 120 s initialization and a further 30 s after ankle-limit action. Initialization itself moves joints. Stay suspended under the approved operator procedure; do not enter Ready/Motion or any diagnostic for this result.
4. Verify existing controls before networking. Use **Unitree Explore**, the enterprise account supplied by Unitree, nearby Bluetooth, `Home → Add Robot`, then robot settings for AP/router Wi-Fi and remote settings. Bind the actual remote ID, R3 right DL or R1 left link indicators. The status subheading says “Go App” in the user manual, but the instructions say Explore; confirm the delivered app revision if the UI differs. View available motor/battery status without using gait buttons. App visibility is not proof of DDS access.
5. Have the responsible engineer confirm the physical user-facing Ethernet route, approved host address/subnet and existing SDK environment, and confirm that **domain 0**, `rt/lowstate` and the `GO` schema are valid on this firmware/edition. Use the supplied approved cable only; the circular power-bearing Ethernet positions are not generic RJ45 pinouts. Identify the robot-facing host interface locally with `ip -br addr` on the approved Linux host; substitute its actual name below. Do not change routes/interfaces or try a guessed address as part of this guide.
6. Save the original code below as `unitree-h1-state-once.py` in that already prepared environment. The conditional invocation is `python3 unitree-h1-state-once.py ROBOT_INTERFACE`, where `ROBOT_INTERFACE` must be replaced by the confirmed local interface. It reads once with a 10 s timeout and closes the subscriber; no mode transition or command publisher is created.

```python
# Original, bounded H1 reader; not a vendor motion example.
# Run only after exact firmware/edition/network approval and physical preparation.
import json
import sys

ACTIVE = {0: 'kRightHipRoll', 1: 'kRightHipPitch', 2: 'kRightKnee', 3: 'kLeftHipRoll', 4: 'kLeftHipPitch', 5: 'kLeftKnee', 6: 'kWaistYaw', 7: 'kLeftHipYaw', 8: 'kRightHipYaw', 10: 'kLeftAnkle', 11: 'kRightAnkle', 12: 'kRightShoulderPitch', 13: 'kRightShoulderRoll', 14: 'kRightShoulderYaw', 15: 'kRightElbow', 16: 'kLeftShoulderPitch', 17: 'kLeftShoulderRoll', 18: 'kLeftShoulderYaw', 19: 'kLeftElbow'}

def main(argv=None):
    args = sys.argv[1:] if argv is None else argv
    if len(args) != 1 or not args[0].strip():
        raise SystemExit("Supply one confirmed robot-facing network interface")
    from unitree_sdk2py.core.channel import ChannelFactoryInitialize, ChannelSubscriber
    from unitree_sdk2py.idl.unitree_go.msg.dds_ import LowState_
    ChannelFactoryInitialize(0, args[0])  # 0 is the pinned example domain, not universal.
    sub = ChannelSubscriber("rt/lowstate", LowState_)
    try:
        sub.Init()  # no callback: use one bounded synchronous read
        msg = sub.Read(10.0)
        if msg is None:
            raise SystemExit("No state in 10 s; check the approved network/schema, do not change control mode")
        if len(msg.motor_state) < 20:
            raise SystemExit("Unexpected motor_state length; stop and recheck model/schema")
        result = {"tick": msg.tick, "power_v": msg.power_v, "power_a": msg.power_a,
                  "joints": {name: {"slot": i, "q_raw": msg.motor_state[i].q,
                                     "dq_raw": msg.motor_state[i].dq}
                             for i, name in ACTIVE.items()}}
        print(json.dumps(result, sort_keys=True))
    finally:
        sub.Close()

if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()
```

**Expected result:** one JSON object with `tick`, `power_v`, `power_a` and 19 named active-joint entries containing source slot and raw `q`/`dq` values. The array must reach slot 19, but slot 9 is deliberately absent from the reported joints. The code is an original integration example; it has not been executed against CycloneDDS, the SDK or a robot. A decoded packet alone does not prove freshness, full sensor health, correct calibration, remote stop authority or permission to move. Record the actual firmware/schema alongside any later log, and stop on wrong model, stale/implausible data or unexpected array shape.

**Diagnostic checkpoint:** if nothing arrives within 10 s, preserve controller state and check the confirmed interface/subnet, approved network path, domain, firmware/edition telemetry permission and exact schema in that order. Do not release a controller, send a pose or invoke Develop/diagnostic mode to make a subscriber succeed. Closing a subscriber only stops observation; it does not stop or shut down the robot.

### Controls, shutdown and recovery bounds <a href="#controls-shutdown-and-recovery-bounds" id="controls-shutdown-and-recovery-bounds"></a>

The [original current remote key table](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/static/2025/4/18/66eefaede61249eaa1b33b95373ddfd1_2482x3509.png) distinguishes damping (`L2+B`), ready from damping (`L2+UP`), motion from ready (`R2+X`), zero torque from damping (`L2+Y`) and Develop (`L2+R2`). Ready changes posture; zero torque removes active support; standing may still step if disturbed. In motion, X/Y decrease/increase standing height, A/B decrease/increase leg lift, and START changes continuous walking/standing. These are explanations for operator identification, **not steps in the subscriber workflow**.

`L2+A` in Develop invokes diagnostic position motion and `L2+B` ends it when that controller accepts the command. H1 remote V2.2 additionally lists L2+LEFT sitting and L1+Y dancing; those require separate controlled commissioning and must not be transferred to H1-2. Old V2.1 double-UP/double-DOWN gesture instructions are superseded by the current SELECT+A/SELECT+Y map.

Before shutdown, restore tension in the approved suspension, maintain stationary support and enter damping under the operator procedure. H1’s pack manual gives brief press then >2 s hold for normal off, applied to both packs as directed by the robot manual. The separate assisted-seated alternative is H1-only. Confirm power off before removing packs, cleaning or packing. Do not rely on a suspended damping state indefinitely: it is still powered and needs supervision.

| Remote full LEDs appear inconsistent         | R3 full is all on; R1 full is all off. Match the physical remote and own-model accessory revision before replacing batteries or assuming a failed charger.                                                                                                                               |
| App cannot discover/bind                     | Check enterprise credentials, Bluetooth permission, proximity and existing owner. Owner-only unbinding is in robot settings; lost accounts go to Unitree. Avoid special characters/spaces in AP configuration as the manual advises; confirm app-version UI differences.                 |
| State subscriber times out                   | Verify the approved network route, explicit host interface, domain 0 applicability and model-specific schema/edition. A visible app or working Wi-Fi does not prove low-state DDS access. No mode switch is justified by timeout alone.                                                  |
| State parses but joint count is wrong        | GO 20 is capacity; H1 has 19 active joints and slot 9 unused. A naïve first-19 slice is wrong; verify exact named mapping and firmware.                                                                                                                                                  |
| Generic GO code reports foot-force fields    | Shared GO schema fields are not evidence that H1 has the same foot sensors as a quadruped. Confirm actual sensor hardware and API meaning before interpretation.                                                                                                                         |
| Unexpected protective braking or instability | Preserve the approved rig and clearance; manufacturer fall protection is not safety certification. Damping can allow collapse. Remove the commissioning cause under supplier guidance; repeated restart/calibration attempts are not recovery validation.                                |
| Need stairs or maximum-speed operation       | Current remote manual excludes stair use. Special-config 3.3 m/s and product potential >5 m/s require a separate verified configuration and protected procedure.                                                                                                                         |
| Battery protection / firmware update failure | H1 battery scan 6 distinguishes normal 1 Hz, protection 2.5 Hz and firmware 5 Hz indications. Check cooling/matched charger safely; do not invent a flash/recovery sequence from the LED state. No verified controller recovery image, rollback or bootloader procedure was established. |

H1’s operating manual describes a forced shutdown for remote failure only while suspended with feet clear and adequate clearance; the accessory additionally documents >15 s forced pack cutoff. Those are last-resort supported conditions, not permission to cut power on a standing robot. If an approved, effective stop/power-removal path is not available, postpone hardware commissioning.
