# Unitree H1: 19-joint hardware, power scope and state-only development

{% hint style="info" %}
[**Unitree H1 — verified OpenELAB product listing**](https://openelab.io/products/unitree-h1-humanoid-robot-with) · SKU `Unitree-H1` · observed catalog has one Default Title option (product `8846453113030`, variant `45731749626054`). This identifies the model, not a guaranteed hand, computer, battery or firmware bundle.
{% endhint %}

Unitree H1 is a full-size humanoid research platform. This guide covers its own hardware, operating manual and a conditional **state-only** SDK2 checkpoint. H1 has 19 active body joints and uses the GO DDS message family; humanoid form does not make it compatible with H1-2’s HG decoder. See the separate [H1-2 guide](unitree-h1-2.md) for the other robot.

## Product overview <a href="#product-overview" id="product-overview"></a>

H1 combines a torso computer, depth/laser sensing, articulated legs and arms, a waist joint and two removable battery packs. Unitree’s [current H1/H1-2 comparative product table](https://www.unitree.com/h1/) and [model-specific overview diagram](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/static/2025/4/18/efd7915fc35f4b4da79e6a81a4723931_2481x3509.png) describe the platform. H1’s single ankle degree of freedom per leg and four-joint arms distinguish it from H1-2’s two-axis ankles and seven-joint arms.

The audited catalog contains exactly one H1 SKU and no separate options for development entitlement, hands, compute, chargers or firmware. A supplier-confirmed serial-number/BOM and controller revision are prerequisites for selecting accessories and commissioning. The manuals describe development functions, but they do not certify that every retail unit exposes the same network/API/control permissions.

### Scope of the now-reviewed H1-family developer collection <a href="#scope-of-the-now-reviewed-h1-family-developer-collection" id="scope-of-the-now-reviewed-h1-family-developer-collection"></a>

H1-specific SLAM and optional Inspire-hand material is kept separate from H1-2 qualification. The original model-specific manual page coverage below remains applicable within its stated revisions.

## Applications <a href="#applications" id="applications"></a>

Use the documented platform for supervised locomotion research, perception integration, state logging and, after a separate approved commissioning process, controller development. State logging is the smallest software result here: receiving one correctly decoded message makes model/interface errors visible before any custom command publisher exists.

The supplied controller manual describes motion, standing and certain H1 gestures; it explicitly excludes current stair operation. Optional hands and an expanded arm are separate configurations, not evidence of a verified manipulation bundle. Neither manual establishes unattended operation, person-carrying, collaborative safety certification or a safe proximity envelope around moving joints. Operators must be at least 18 and trained on the exact configuration. Keep line of sight, a prepared frame and the model’s operating clearances.

### Development tasks and their prerequisites <a href="#development-tasks-and-their-prerequisites" id="development-tasks-and-their-prerequisites"></a>

The developer chapters support several different projects: recording motor/IMU/remote state; designing a low-level controller; commanding the built-in locomotion service; blending arm targets into that service; and, on the documented H1 configuration, building a LiDAR map and topological route. These tasks require different ownership, sensors and software. A receive-only logger neither needs nor establishes permission for an active controller. A successful SDK request is evidence about that request, not task completion, balance or an independently effective stop. [Service overview](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/10/192/en/10_192_en), [basic service](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/10/193/en/10_193_en), [motion-service overview](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/10/196/en/10_196_en).

### Revision and interpretation boundaries <a href="#revision-and-interpretation-boundaries" id="revision-and-interpretation-boundaries"></a>

The manufacturer’s aggregate 864 Wh/maximum 67.2 V row and the accessory’s 432 Wh/33.6 V charging figure refer to different scopes. Two packs are shown in the robot, but the reviewed sources do not establish the electrical topology; do not infer a series/parallel harness or a 67.2 V pack charger.

## Hardware and connections <a href="#hardware-and-connections" id="hardware-and-connections"></a>

### Support, reference posture and two-pack installation <a href="#support-reference-posture-and-two-pack-installation" id="support-reference-posture-and-two-pack-installation"></a>

The [original suspension figure](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/static/2025/4/18/cd7a9f331eff439f9ae7a3e1dd2fc7ba_2482x3509.png) uses the designated buckles on **both shoulders** and a protective frame with its base/castors secured, keeping feet clear during initialization. The source does not specify a working-load rating or certify a generic hoist. Obtain the approved rig and operator procedure for the delivered robot; do not lift by hands, sensor head, cable looms or arbitrary torso covers.

The [startup reference-posture and latch figure](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/static/2025/4/18/8f19170c7c1441778b00bdcc630d28d0_2481x3509.png) shows the arms brought inward to their limits, then hanging vertically, with forefeet lifted to the stated limit. These positions establish arm/ankle zero references at startup; incorrect placement can produce unexpected motion. Manipulate posture only under the manual’s unpowered/supported conditions, keeping hands clear before energizing. H1 V1.1 additionally describes assisted seated startup/shutdown. That procedure requires its own seat/support preparation and is not a substitute for the suspension workflow used here.

Insert both **off** packs from the side, switches toward the robot’s front, and confirm each latch clicks and is seated. Correct orientation rather than forcing an incompletely seated pack. Manufacturer quick-swap language is not proof of hot-swap capability: power down and establish support before removal. A battery connector’s mechanical fit is not cross-model electrical compatibility.

### Battery, charger and controller handling <a href="#battery-charger-and-controller-handling" id="battery-charger-and-controller-handling"></a>

The H1-only battery V1.0 [original rating table](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/static/2024/8/15/0cc0ecae04344a689a65527a1784ae20_2481x3508.png) specifies each pack at **28.8 V nominal, 15 Ah/432 Wh, charge limit 33.6 V**, size 120 × 80 × 182 mm. The listed charger is 154 × 60 × 36 mm, input 100–240 V AC, 50/60 Hz, 4 A/350 VA, output **33.6 V, 9 A, 302.4 W**, with about 1.5 h source charge time. Check actual matching nameplates; do not use the aggregate robot maximum voltage to select a charger.

The [pack power and charge diagram](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/static/2024/8/15/1877f49946684e7d8e821b60ccb3e6a3_2482x3508.png) gives a brief press followed by a hold longer than 2 s for normal on/off. The user manual calls for both pack buttons together on startup. A hold longer than 15 s is the accessory’s forced-cutoff operation, not routine startup troubleshooting. Remove and turn off packs for charging, connect the original charger to matching AC first, then the pack, after cooling. Supervise in a dry, ventilated noncombustible location; ideal charging is 5–40 °C, while the BMS charge-protection thresholds are below 0 °C or above 50 °C. Protection thresholds are not target operating temperatures.

The BMS document describes balancing, overcharge/short protection, charge interruption above 15 A, discharge cutoff at 24 V and idle self-discharge to 65% after ten days above 65%. These are internal protection descriptions, **not** supply/current settings or a reason to bypass shutdown. Stop use below the manual’s 40% recommendation. Its −15 to 55 °C pack-use range does not expand the robot’s 0–40 °C environment. Store removed packs dry/cool (22–28 °C recommended), avoid above 40 °C, and restore to 70% when below 30%. Damaged/wet/impacted packs require supplier service and appropriate recycling, not disassembly or improvised discharge. Manufacturer transport-state advice does not establish carrier/regulatory permission.

The exact-model remote manual covers **R3 and older R1**; identify the physical remote first. R3: 780 mAh, nominal about 5 h, listed 5 V/700 mA supply parameter, with off-state USB-C charging from 5 V/2 A; full-charge LEDs are **all on**. R1: 2500 mAh, about 4.5 h, 5 V/2 A, and full-charge LEDs **all off**. Both quote >100 m in an open environment, not a guaranteed indoor link. See [R3 parameters](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/static/2025/4/18/c3f46e3e080e4a89a53250c9e282ebed_2482x3509.png), [R3 charging/binding](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/static/2025/4/18/f019a909a90a4a80b2ee49ceda8b7bb0_2481x3509.png), [R1 parameters](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/static/2025/4/18/be8de50c52534ebfa0940f76272b7033_2481x3509.png) and [R1 charging](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/static/2025/4/18/292640a1b80c4be0beb981a22c9768cd_2481x3509.png). These accessory values are supported by this model’s own remote document.

For stick drift, the remote document uses F1+F3 while sticks are untouched, then full-range stick movement and F3 to save after beeping stops. Calibrate the remote with the robot safely supported and no active motion command path. R3 feedback is changed by triple F3 (sound/vibration) and triple F1 (on/off); holding F1 during shutdown saves the preference. An unconnected, idle R3 may turn off after ten minutes. Remote sound, link LEDs and battery LEDs describe different conditions.

### DDS, RPC and version-matched generated types <a href="#dds-rpc-and-version-matched-generated-types" id="dds-rpc-and-version-matched-generated-types"></a>

SDK2 wraps DDS publish/subscribe channels and request/response services. Continuous state is published by the robot and subscribed to by the application. RPC responses are matched to requests; a response is not an independent physical-state measurement. The cloud/app architecture describes BLE for network setup/authentication, WebRTC for app media/state/control, MQTT for monitoring/OTA/signaling, HTTP for account/device binding, and STUN/TURN for peer connectivity or relay. The architecture's user-authorization and data-handling statements are manufacturer descriptions, not an independent privacy audit. Develop only on the designated user computer; leave PC1's platform services intact. [Architecture](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/10/190/en/10_190_en), [SDK overview](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/10/171/en/10_171_en).

The [DDS service chapter](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/10/175/en/10_175_en) documents singleton factory initialization, typed send/receive channels, publisher `Write`, subscriber initialization/close and callback queues. Long processing belongs outside the DDS callback; a nonzero queue can decouple reception and processing but may accumulate older data. `GetLastDataAvailableTime()` is described in monotonic microseconds since startup, with `−1` before initialization; it is not a Unix timestamp or proof of synchronized sensor acquisition. Factory `Release()` frees transport resources and does not stop motors.

At the pinned SDK2 commit, use the actual **two-argument** `ChannelFactory::Instance()->Init(domain, interface)` form. The portal's extra `enableSharedMemory` argument is not in that pinned header. Its subscriber paragraph incorrectly mentions `CreateSendChannel`; the actual subscriber uses `CreateRecvChannel`. The portal also sketches `GetServerApiVersion()` returning a string reference, while pinned `client.hpp` returns `std::string` by value. These are documentation/source-version mismatches, not reasons to modify the working state-reader code. H1's documented body transport is `unitree_go::msg::dds_`, with **20 motor-array entries and 19 physical joints**. ### Optional H1 hands: DFX and DFTP are separate protocols <a href="#optional-h1-hands-dfx-and-dftp-are-separate-protocols" id="optional-h1-hands-dfx-and-dftp-are-separate-protocols"></a>

RH56DFX has six actuators and twelve moving joints per hand. The Unitree-owned [DFX bridge](https://github.com/unitreerobotics/dfx_inspire_service/tree/d6c4eae9bc959990bf87c262f29f62142519e923) exposes GO `MotorCmds_`/`MotorStates_` on `rt/inspire/cmd` / `rt/inspire/state`, with twelve entries for both hands: **right 0–5, left 6–11**, ordered pinky, ring, middle, index, thumb bend, thumb rotation. Only `q` is meaningful in the chapter; it is normalized **0 closed / 1 open**, not radians. Other command fields are reserved. The USB/RS-485 device path is installation-specific; `/dev/ttyUSB0` is an example, not a universal serial identity. Check real receive timestamps and vector lengths before accepting getter values; zero-initialized local state is not received telemetry. [DFX chapter](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/10/200/en/10_200_en).

RH56DFTP instead offers Modbus RTU over RS-485 or Modbus TCP, with tactile data only on the TCP route in this source. Its **17 tactile modules** and six actuator values per side use custom `inspire` control/state/touch definitions and side suffixes `l`/`r`. The chapter's broad twelve-motor prose must not replace the actual per-side six-element fields. Control mode is a bitmap: angle `1`, position `2`, force `4`, speed `8`; combinations 0–15 select fields. Position/angle/force/current/error/status/temperature value units need the exact hand protocol, and DFX's normalized `q` does not apply. Tactile sequences have different lengths by module, including 9/96-element fingertip/segment groups, 80-element finger palm regions and a 112-element palm; do not decode them as a uniform motor array. [DFTP chapter](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/10/302/en/10_302_en).

The DFTP chapter links the **third-party** [NaCl-1374 workspace](https://github.com/NaCl-1374/inspire_hand_ws/tree/fc754900caaaa82c9b59fb12c1b79ebfd1c1a0e7), not a Unitree-owned repository. It uses SDK2 Python plus separate hand definitions/UI dependencies. Reference TCP addresses are `.124.210` / right `.124.211`, port `6000`, device ID `1`; serial examples use 115200 baud and report about 20 Hz on one bus. These are example configurations, not guaranteed delivered addresses/rates. The printed `192.1686.11.210` is malformed. Changing IP/hand IDs, saving settings and restarting are persistent mutations; a driver can receive DDS commands and write to the hand, so its name does not make it passive. Neither hand chapter proves H1-2 mechanical, power or controller compatibility.

Source inspection of the pinned DFX bridge adds important operational boundaries: it opens the RS-485 serial path at 115200 baud, uses right/left device IDs 1/2 and a 10000 µs recurrent loop, and can write received commands to both hands. Failed position reads increment `lost` while the previously held position can still be published; require current receive/transaction evidence instead of assuming every output position is newly measured. The lower serial wrapper uses frame header `0xEB,0x90`, little-endian payload words and an eight-bit additive checksum over bytes 2 through the byte before the checksum. It scales normalized position by 1000 and clamps 0–1000. Although a comment says `−1` means no change, the actual shown conversion clamps a negative value to 0, so no sentinel behavior is prescribed here. Force-setting comments use grams; the read wrapper converts its reported gram-based value to newtons using 9.8 m/s². This is source conversion logic, not a calibrated force measurement or hand-force limit. `ChangeID`, `ClearError` and unloaded-force `Calibration` are explicit hardware writes, excluded from a first check. [Pinned serial wrapper](https://github.com/unitreerobotics/dfx_inspire_service/blob/d6c4eae9bc959990bf87c262f29f62142519e923/include/inspire.h), [H1 bridge](https://github.com/unitreerobotics/dfx_inspire_service/blob/d6c4eae9bc959990bf87c262f29f62142519e923/inspire_h1.cpp).

The pinned third-party DFTP `ModbusDataHandler` is also **active during construction**: after connecting/initializing DDS it writes register 1004=1 to reset errors, creates state/touch publishers and subscribes to control. Its `network` branch drops a nonempty requested interface rather than forwarding it, so it is not an accepted interface-binding example. The selected headless-left example uses `.123.211` with suffix `l`, whereas the portal examples use `.124.210/.211` and the workspace guide also mentions `.11.210`. Those are conflicting example/configuration records, not interchangeable default addresses or side detection. The source reads six signed 16-bit state words, splits byte fields from register high/low bytes, and routes command bitmap 1/2/4/8 to angle/position/force/speed registers 1486/1474/1498/1522. Failed reads can yield `None`, which must not be accepted as valid telemetry. A C++ example publishes changing angle commands scaled to 1000; it is not a read-only subscriber despite printing status. [Pinned DFTP driver](https://github.com/NaCl-1374/inspire_hand_ws/blob/fc754900caaaa82c9b59fb12c1b79ebfd1c1a0e7/inspire_hand_sdk/inspire_sdkpy/inspire_sdk.py), [example side/address](https://github.com/NaCl-1374/inspire_hand_ws/blob/fc754900caaaa82c9b59fb12c1b79ebfd1c1a0e7/inspire_hand_sdk/example/Headless_driver_l.py).

## Getting started <a href="#getting-started" id="getting-started"></a>

### First result: one model-specific state message, conditionally <a href="#first-result-one-model-specific-state-message-conditionally" id="first-result-one-model-specific-state-message-conditionally"></a>

This workflow is for an already authorized development unit with an existing approved SDK2 host environment. It is not permission to install software, flash firmware, wire a custom harness or energize an unsupported robot. If the edition/firmware/network contract is not confirmed, stop after the physical/document identity checkpoint; the draft remains useful without claiming telemetry was obtained.

1. Record `Unitree-H1`, robot serial, exact pack/charger IDs, remote R1/R3 revision, installed computers/sensors/hands, firmware and current controller ownership. Confirm this is H1, not the other H1-family robot. Match the H1 pack and charger labels to the separate accessory document before charging.
2. Have a trained operator prepare the approved protective frame with secured base, both shoulder attachments and feet clear. Inspect connectors, latches, foot rubber, optics and fans, and establish the exact startup reference posture from the linked diagram. Use a dry 0–40 °C, flat/high-traction, observed work area with at least 2 m clearance. Clear personnel before energizing.
3. With two correctly fitted off packs, the own-model startup procedure is a brief press of both power buttons together followed by holding both for more than 2 s. Allow about 120 s initialization and a further 30 s after ankle-limit action. Initialization itself moves joints. Stay suspended under the approved operator procedure; do not enter Ready/Motion or any diagnostic for this result.
4. Verify existing controls before networking. Use **Unitree Explore**, the enterprise account supplied by Unitree, nearby Bluetooth, `Home → Add Robot`, then robot settings for AP/router Wi-Fi and remote settings. Bind the actual remote ID, R3 right DL or R1 left link indicators. The status subheading says “Go App” in the user manual, but the instructions say Explore; confirm the delivered app revision if the UI differs. View available motor/battery status without using gait buttons. App visibility is not proof of DDS access.
5. Have the responsible engineer confirm the physical user-facing Ethernet route, approved host address/subnet and existing SDK environment, and confirm that **domain 0**, `rt/lowstate` and the `GO` schema are valid on this firmware/edition. Use the supplied approved cable only; the circular power-bearing Ethernet positions are not generic RJ45 pinouts. Identify the robot-facing host interface locally with `ip -br addr` on the approved Linux host; substitute its actual name below. Do not change routes/interfaces or try a guessed address as part of this guide.
6. Save the original code below as `unitree-h1-state-once.py` in that already prepared environment. The conditional invocation is `python3 unitree-h1-state-once.py ROBOT_INTERFACE`, where `ROBOT_INTERFACE` must be replaced by the confirmed local interface. It reads once with a 10 s timeout and closes the subscriber; no mode transition or command publisher is created.

```python
# Original, bounded H1 reader; not a vendor motion example.
# Run only after exact firmware/edition/network approval and physical preparation.
import json
import sys

ACTIVE = {0: 'kRightHipRoll', 1: 'kRightHipPitch', 2: 'kRightKnee', 3: 'kLeftHipRoll', 4: 'kLeftHipPitch', 5: 'kLeftKnee', 6: 'kWaistYaw', 7: 'kLeftHipYaw', 8: 'kRightHipYaw', 10: 'kLeftAnkle', 11: 'kRightAnkle', 12: 'kRightShoulderPitch', 13: 'kRightShoulderRoll', 14: 'kRightShoulderYaw', 15: 'kRightElbow', 16: 'kLeftShoulderPitch', 17: 'kLeftShoulderRoll', 18: 'kLeftShoulderYaw', 19: 'kLeftElbow'}

def main(argv=None):
    args = sys.argv[1:] if argv is None else argv
    if len(args) != 1 or not args[0].strip():
        raise SystemExit("Supply one confirmed robot-facing network interface")
    from unitree_sdk2py.core.channel import ChannelFactoryInitialize, ChannelSubscriber
    from unitree_sdk2py.idl.unitree_go.msg.dds_ import LowState_
    ChannelFactoryInitialize(0, args[0])  # 0 is the pinned example domain, not universal.
    sub = ChannelSubscriber("rt/lowstate", LowState_)
    try:
        sub.Init()  # no callback: use one bounded synchronous read
        msg = sub.Read(10.0)
        if msg is None:
            raise SystemExit("No state in 10 s; check the approved network/schema, do not change control mode")
        if len(msg.motor_state) < 20:
            raise SystemExit("Unexpected motor_state length; stop and recheck model/schema")
        result = {"tick": msg.tick, "power_v": msg.power_v, "power_a": msg.power_a,
                  "joints": {name: {"slot": i, "q_raw": msg.motor_state[i].q,
                                     "dq_raw": msg.motor_state[i].dq}
                             for i, name in ACTIVE.items()}}
        print(json.dumps(result, sort_keys=True))
    finally:
        sub.Close()

if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()
```

**Expected result:** one JSON object with `tick`, `power_v`, `power_a` and 19 named active-joint entries containing source slot and raw `q`/`dq` values. The array must reach slot 19, but slot 9 is deliberately absent from the reported joints. The code is an original integration example; it has not been executed against CycloneDDS, the SDK or a robot. A decoded packet alone does not prove freshness, full sensor health, correct calibration, remote stop authority or permission to move. Record the actual firmware/schema alongside any later log, and stop on wrong model, stale/implausible data or unexpected array shape.

**Diagnostic checkpoint:** if nothing arrives within 10 s, preserve controller state and check the confirmed interface/subnet, approved network path, domain, firmware/edition telemetry permission and exact schema in that order. Do not release a controller, send a pose or invoke Develop/diagnostic mode to make a subscriber succeed. Closing a subscriber only stops observation; it does not stop or shut down the robot.

### Controls, shutdown and recovery bounds <a href="#controls-shutdown-and-recovery-bounds" id="controls-shutdown-and-recovery-bounds"></a>

The [original current remote key table](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/static/2025/4/18/66eefaede61249eaa1b33b95373ddfd1_2482x3509.png) distinguishes damping (`L2+B`), ready from damping (`L2+UP`), motion from ready (`R2+X`), zero torque from damping (`L2+Y`) and Develop (`L2+R2`). Ready changes posture; zero torque removes active support; standing may still step if disturbed. In motion, X/Y decrease/increase standing height, A/B decrease/increase leg lift, and START changes continuous walking/standing. These are explanations for operator identification, **not steps in the subscriber workflow**.

`L2+A` in Develop invokes diagnostic position motion and `L2+B` ends it when that controller accepts the command. H1 remote V2.2 additionally lists L2+LEFT sitting and L1+Y dancing; those require separate controlled commissioning and must not be transferred to H1-2. Old V2.1 double-UP/double-DOWN gesture instructions are superseded by the current SELECT+A/SELECT+Y map.

Before shutdown, restore tension in the approved suspension, maintain stationary support and enter damping under the operator procedure. H1’s pack manual gives brief press then >2 s hold for normal off, applied to both packs as directed by the robot manual. The separate assisted-seated alternative is H1-only. Confirm power off before removing packs, cleaning or packing. Do not rely on a suspended damping state indefinitely: it is still powered and needs supervision.

### Remote payload and observation timing <a href="#remote-payload-and-observation-timing" id="remote-payload-and-observation-timing"></a>

The remote-data chapter defines a **40-byte** packet: two-byte header, 16-bit button union, float fields `lx`, `rx`, `ry`, `L2`, `ly`, then sixteen idle bytes. Button-bit order is `R1`, `L1`, `start`, `select`, `R2`, `L2`, `F1`, `F2`, `A`, `B`, `X`, `Y`, `up`, `right`, `down`, `left`. Stick values span −1 to +1. Preserve packing/endian assumptions and compare the actual generated message before interpreting bytes; the structure's existence is not a remote-control command or approved stop path. Log local monotonic receive time alongside device `tick`, firmware, mode and physical-joint names. Device ticks, DDS arrival and media timestamps have different clock/latency meanings. [Remote data](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/10/182/en/10_182_en).

| State subscriber times out                   | Verify the approved network route, explicit host interface, domain 0 applicability and model-specific schema/edition. A naïve first-19 slice is wrong; verify exact named mapping and firmware.                                                                                                                                                  |

| Need stairs or maximum-speed operation       | Current remote manual excludes stair use. Special-config 3.3 m/s and product potential >5 m/s require a separate verified configuration and protected procedure.                                                                                                                         |
| Battery protection / firmware update failure | H1 battery scan 6 distinguishes normal 1 Hz, protection 2.5 Hz and firmware 5 Hz indications. H1’s operating manual describes a forced shutdown for remote failure only while suspended with feet clear and adequate clearance; the accessory additionally documents >15 s forced pack cutoff. Those are last-resort supported conditions, not permission to cut power on a standing robot. If an approved, effective stop/power-removal path is not available, postpone hardware commissioning.

### Distinguish service errors from physical faults <a href="#distinguish-service-errors-from-physical-faults" id="distinguish-service-errors-from-physical-faults"></a>

| Code family            | Interpretation and next evidence                                                                                        |
| ---------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `3001`                 | Unclassified client/server failure; retain the operation, return code and versions                                      |
| `3102`, `3103`, `3104` | Request sending failed, API unregistered, request timed out; inspect host transport/API pairing before another request  |
| `3105`, `3106`, `3107` | Request/response mismatch, invalid response, invalid lease; retain request identity and actual decoder/client version   |
| `3201`                 | Server response-send failure; documented as not returned to the client                                                  |
| `3202`–`3207`          | Server internal error, unimplemented API, bad parameters, rejected request, invalid lease, existing lease, respectively |

A retry, service toggle or firmware change is not a repair for an unsupported API or a mechanical/electrical fault. Preserve raw device fault words with their source, motor ID, family/revision and timestamp. A fault code is a **bitmask**, so several faults can be active together. A JSON `errors` list uses `[timestamp, source, code]`; the chapter's sample structure does not by itself identify a universal aggregate-fault topic. Subscribe to the actual model-specific source topics listed below. [DDS errors](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/10/175/en/10_175_en).

### H1 fault-source and motor-family lookup <a href="#h1-fault-source-and-motor-family-lookup" id="h1-fault-source-and-motor-family-lookup"></a>

The [H1 fault chapter](https://doc-cdn.unitree.com/10/270/en/10_270_en) identifies source `100` on `rt/lf/lowstate`, reading `bit_flag`. It maps `0x01` command timeout, `0x02` motor overcurrent, `0x10` battery communication, `0x40` MCU communication and `0x80` motor communication. **`0x20` conflicts:** this chapter calls it joint-start anomaly, while Basic Services calls it remote timeout. Preserve the raw flag and matching firmware definition rather than selecting a meaning by convenience.

Motor source is `300 + motorID`, reading `motor_state[motorID].reserve[0]`; `reserve[1]` is described as that motor's current communication frequency. The two families have different meanings:

| Mask                         | Core-family description                                           | Ankle/arm family, IDs `10–19`                                                  |
| ---------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `0x01`                       | Overcurrent                                                       | Overcurrent                                                                    |
| `0x02`                       | Phase leakage                                                     | Overvoltage                                                                    |
| `0x04`                       | MOS over-temperature                                              | Driver over-temperature                                                        |
| `0x08`, `0x10`               | Bus undervoltage / winding overheat                               | Same descriptions                                                              |
| `0x20`                       | Encoder communication                                             | Encoder anomaly                                                                |
| `0x40`                       | Encoder anomaly                                                   | Not assigned here                                                              |
| `0x100`, `0x1000`, `0x10000` | Motor communication interrupted / command anomaly / state anomaly | Not assigned here                                                              |
| `0x40000000`, `0x80000000`   | Not assigned in this core table                                   | Two directional disconnection-timeout entries; confirm actual endpoint meaning |

Do not decode H1-2 HG `motorstate` using these GO reserved-word masks. A missing communication update can also leave old values in memory; retain source age, device tick and raw fields. Secure/support the robot and retain logs before any supplier-directed repair or controlled restart. No fault-clearing, calibration or power-cycle command is implied by this lookup.
