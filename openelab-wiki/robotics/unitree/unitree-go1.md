# Unitree Go1 Air, Pro and EDU: legacy SDK, interfaces and manual guide

{% hint style="info" %}
**Manufacturer reference: Unitree Go1 Air, Pro and EDU.** An exact OpenELAB robot listing and SKU have not been verified. The Go1 battery accessory is a separate product. This guide supports document and source inspection; charging and physical commissioning require the delivered robot's matching instructions.
{% endhint %}

Unitree Go1 is a 12-motor quadruped with edition-dependent cameras, compute and development access. Its legacy UDP interface belongs to the Go1 SDK line, rather than SDK2 used by newer robots. For maintaining an existing system, the important first decision is the exact Air/Pro/EDU configuration and software image, not which repository happens to contain a similarly named header. [Official Go1 model and edition table](https://www.unitree.com/go1)

## Product overview <a href="#product-overview" id="product-overview"></a>

The manufacturer distinguishes intelligent side following, binocular perception, joint drives, onboard compute and app/web management. These are separate subsystems: an image arriving from a camera does not establish motion-controller connectivity, and successful radio-tag following does not establish obstacle avoidance. Development access is marked for EDU in the edition table; Air and Pro should not be assumed to expose the same scientific-research or Python interface. [Go1 features and edition comparison](https://www.unitree.com/go1)

The older manuals describe Raspberry Pi and NVIDIA boards in a distributed architecture. Their board counts and connector assignments are configuration examples, not proof that every Air, Pro or EDU has the illustrated hardware. Identify the robot label, controller hardware revision, installed boards, battery, remote and approved charger before planning a connection.

## Applications <a href="#applications" id="applications"></a>

* **EDU control and perception research:** inspect high-level body commands separately from low-level joint commands, and use the correct MCU or sport-controller path. These interfaces have different endpoints and control authority.
* **Camera and calibration work:** distinguish raw, rectified and depth images, point clouds and camera calibration parameters. Five binocular pairs are an EDU/Pro specification, while the software camera-development chapter specifically describes EDU resources.
* **Following demonstrations:** the side-following system needs a suitable unobstructed route and tag position. Loss of radio positioning, poor light, low texture and ultrasonic interference remain relevant limitations.
* **Existing fleet diagnosis:** the user manual describes black-box records of inertial measurements, joints, contact forces and commands. Analyze exported records before attempting to reproduce a fault physically. [User manual pp.23–35](https://docs.trossenrobotics.com/unitree_go1_docs/_downloads/570715163583be1c0182fb6d4f3b7413/go1_user_manual.pdf#page=23), [Software manual pp.3–13](https://docs.trossenrobotics.com/unitree_go1_docs/_downloads/23a15b8ec933927ef4ee1ba8f70abba6/go1_software_manual.pdf#page=3)

## Hardware and connections <a href="#hardware-and-connections" id="hardware-and-connections"></a>

The old software diagram separates an MCU Basic controller at `192.168.123.10`, a Pi Sport controller at `192.168.123.161`, and perception boards at `.13`, `.14` and `.15`. These are addresses in that document, not discovered addresses on your network. The 2021 software chapter gives a sport endpoint at `.161:8082` and a low-level endpoint at `.10:8007`. The current SDK examples corroborate those distinct targets; do not configure both as one service. [Architecture figure, software p.3](https://docs.trossenrobotics.com/unitree_go1_docs/_downloads/23a15b8ec933927ef4ee1ba8f70abba6/go1_software_manual.pdf#page=3), [network/API chapters, pp.4–8](https://docs.trossenrobotics.com/unitree_go1_docs/_downloads/23a15b8ec933927ef4ee1ba8f70abba6/go1_software_manual.pdf#page=4)

The [user interface figure, p.45](https://docs.trossenrobotics.com/unitree_go1_docs/_downloads/570715163583be1c0182fb6d4f3b7413/go1_user_manual.pdf#page=45) identifies USB/HDMI pairs, gigabit Ethernet, SIM, integrated and Type-C interfaces and an external **24 V, 30 A input label**. The [software figure, p.3](https://docs.trossenrobotics.com/unitree_go1_docs/_downloads/23a15b8ec933927ef4ee1ba8f70abba6/go1_software_manual.pdf#page=3) assigns some Nano board numbers differently. The external input is not the battery charger; USB appearance is not a power-input specification. Connector pinout, polarity, cable rating and delivered board numbering have not been qualified, so no external-power wiring is provided. The documents also mention 5/12/19 V outputs without establishing every connector's safe load budget.

Before any later qualified live session, obtain the matching mechanical protection, startup, battery and stop instructions. User V1.4 describes automatic standing during startup and a remote OFF action that cuts distribution power and can cause a fall. It explicitly warns remote commands conflict with high-level development and are ineffective during low-level development. A software “stop” or valid camera stream cannot establish a supported braking hold or functioning emergency stop. The protective-frame guide gives assembly diagrams, not a certified fall-arrest rating. [User pp.11,19–21,35–36](https://docs.trossenrobotics.com/unitree_go1_docs/_downloads/570715163583be1c0182fb6d4f3b7413/go1_user_manual.pdf#page=11), [frame guide](https://docs.trossenrobotics.com/unitree_go1_docs/_downloads/a872a30d7ba85e32a7aebf9ec35e51f8/go1_protective_frame_assembly_guide.pdf)

Foot pads contain pressure-sensing air paths and left/right parts; air-tube condition affects contact estimates. Wear, noise and leakage need the matching service procedure rather than a guessed recalibration command. Battery storage, shipping and warranty chapters are indexed below; their historical charge-percentage recommendations differ and are not instructions to force-discharge a pack.

## Getting started <a href="#getting-started" id="getting-started"></a>

### First result: inspect the Go1 source contract without a robot <a href="#first-result-inspect-the-go1-source-contract-without-a-robot" id="first-result-inspect-the-go1-source-contract-without-a-robot"></a>

Prerequisites: a browser and the pinned public files below. Leave robot networking and power disconnected. This checkpoint identifies a source contract; it neither decodes a captured packet nor establishes physical communication.

1. Open the [v3.8.6 README at the pinned commit](https://github.com/unitreerobotics/unitree_legged_sdk/blob/4539a6c10dfbc9781cea6fcb7d51bc6ddc6f71e1/README.md). Find `support robot: Go1` and the explicit exclusions. Record the commit with the robot's proposed software baseline; if your model is A1/AlienGo/Laikago, stop this workflow.
2. Open [the same commit's `comm.h`](https://github.com/unitreerobotics/unitree_legged_sdk/blob/4539a6c10dfbc9781cea6fcb7d51bc6ddc6f71e1/include/unitree_legged_sdk/comm.h). Find `HIGHLEVEL`, `LOWLEVEL`, `LowState`, `tick` and `motorState`. Confirm `0xee`, `0xff`, the ms comment and 20-slot storage. Do not infer 20 motors.
3. Compare [SDK 3.3.1 `comm.h`](https://github.com/unitreerobotics/unitree_legged_sdk/blob/20929b54030c0d8ca4e7ac49ec2a02c447cb9fcd/include/unitree_legged_sdk/comm.h). Confirm its `HIGHLEVEL=0x00` and microsecond tick comment. A decoder or log converter that assumes these contracts are identical fails this check.
4. Read the pinned [walking example](https://github.com/unitreerobotics/unitree_legged_sdk/blob/4539a6c10dfbc9781cea6fcb7d51bc6ddc6f71e1/example/example_walk.cpp). Locate the UDP target and the timed mode/velocity assignments. Classify it as an actuator example before considering any future use.
5. Record the result as `Go1 / SDK 3.8.6 / HIGHLEVEL 0xee / tick ms / 12 physical joints, 20 state slots / physical link not tested`.

**Expected result:** the support scope, state units and example's control authority can be located in actual official source files. No software is installed and no packet is sent. If values differ, first check the URL commit and filename; if the installed robot image remains unknown, retain the mismatch and obtain its release contract rather than trying another decoder on hardware.

For later exported-log analysis, reject missing/zero-initialized state as a first sample, require a subsequent advancing controller tick and a fresh local receive timestamp, and reject stale, backward or nonfinite samples. Tick rollover and reconnect/reset need explicit handling. A receiver returning successfully or repeating an old structure is insufficient. These are engineering acceptance criteria, not a claim that the SDK implements them automatically.

## Troubleshooting and FAQs <a href="#troubleshooting-and-faqs" id="troubleshooting-and-faqs"></a>

**Why does an example compile while the robot is unsupported?** First read the pinned README; then inspect its selected model and controller target. Headers and enums can outlive support. Compilation does not validate model, firmware, hardware architecture or permission to control it.

**Why is a camera visible but state is missing?** Separate perception from MCU/sport traffic. Compare the actual on-unit network record with software pp.3–8, check for address collisions and classify the service being observed. Do not change interfaces or send motion merely to obtain a reply.

**Why are joint angles or foot forces implausible?** Check decoder version, SI units, FR/FL/RR/RL order and hip/thigh/calf index order, then sample freshness. The software manual explains mathematical zero and force drift; its calf zero need not be physically reachable. Mechanical air-path faults can also affect contact readings. Do not use a bad estimate for live control. [Software pp.5–7](https://docs.trossenrobotics.com/unitree_go1_docs/_downloads/23a15b8ec933927ef4ee1ba8f70abba6/go1_software_manual.pdf#page=5)

**Does following guarantee obstacle avoidance?** No. The old tag chapter says an obstacle-avoidance feature was not enabled, while product features vary by edition and image. The tag and app controls are described as mutually exclusive. Confirm the actual enabled feature; do not treat tracking loss as an emergency-stop mechanism. [User pp.23–28](https://docs.trossenrobotics.com/unitree_go1_docs/_downloads/570715163583be1c0182fb6d4f3b7413/go1_user_manual.pdf#page=23)

**Can the edition-table charger be substituted for a manual charger?** No substitution is established. Resolve the 24 V label, 25.2 V charge table and 4 A/6 A discrepancy with the actual approved pack/charger instructions. A battery accessory listing does not resolve robot electrical compatibility.
