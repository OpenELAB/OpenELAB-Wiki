## Product overview <a href="#product-overview" id="product-overview"></a>

The quick-start excludes use by people under 16 and cautions against child contact. Do not disassemble or modify the optical/electrical assembly; no user-repairable internals are identified. L1 is a 230 g non-repetitive time-of-flight scanning LiDAR with an integrated accelerometer/gyroscope and a 12 V, 3.3 V-TTL UART connection. It supplies measurements; it does not itself store maps, export datasets, or implement autonomous navigation. “4D” means 3D position plus reflected-light intensity, not Doppler velocity. The rotating mirror and slower rotating measurement core cover a hemisphere with uneven spatial density. More points near the center and a denser accumulated display do not imply uniform instantaneous coverage. [Manufacturer L1](https://www.unitree.com/LiDAR/), [official developer introduction](https://support.unitree.com/home/en/L1_SDK/L1_introduce).

The manufacturer distinguishes **PM** (20 m at 90% reflectivity / 10 m at 10%) and **RM** (30 m / 15 m). The observed store option names are “Close version” and “Long distance version”; the listing does not establish an exact PM/RM part-number mapping. Obtain the delivered label and range variant before choosing processing limits. Both store options belong here; neither is a second canonical product guide. L2 is a separate product with different power wiring, protocol, rates, and mode behavior.

## Applications <a href="#applications" id="applications"></a>

L1 supports stationary scene capture, low-speed mobile mapping, navigation research, and LiDAR-inertial odometry. Application software must handle transport integrity, scan assembly, time, extrinsics, and storage. Its small body and low typical power are useful on compact robots; they do not establish a safety-rated protective scanner or reliable detection of every glass, dark, reflective, fog-obscured, or thin target. Reflectivity-qualified ranges and the near-zone accuracy exclusion matter when specifying coverage.

The official [Point-LIO adaptation](https://github.com/unitreerobotics/point_lio_unilidar) is an additional algorithm resource, not a prerequisite for receiving the first cloud. Start with a stationary sensor and known geometry before adding odometry. Record whether points use device time or a host-derived clock, the chosen scan aggregation, and the measured sensor-to-base transform. This guide does not validate a mobile robot, a Point-LIO build, or any robot's delivered LiDAR bill of materials.

## Hardware and connections <a href="#hardware-and-connections" id="hardware-and-connections"></a>

### Four-pin power and serial interface <a href="#four-pin-power-and-serial-interface" id="four-pin-power-and-serial-interface"></a>

| Official pin | Signal / wire | Meaning                                     |
| ------------ | ------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| 1            | VCC / red     | 12 V DC sensor supply                       |
| 2            | GND / white   | Common power/serial reference               |
| 3            | RX / green    | Sensor input, 3.3 V logic; connect host TX  |
| 4            | TX / black    | Sensor output, 3.3 V logic; connect host RX |

The socket is GH1.25 mm, 4 pin. **L1's pin 1 is powered; L2's visually similar serial branch has an unused pin 1. SV1-25 uses USB and 5 V. None is interchangeable.** Do not connect RS232 voltage levels, 5 V logic, or an Ethernet cable to the L1 UART. Use the model-matched supplied converter, power adapter, and USB data cable. USB enumeration alone does not prove the LiDAR has its separate 12 V supply. Disconnect power before handling or changing a harness. [Interface definition and original connector drawing](https://support.unitree.com/home/en/L1_SDK/L1_Overview_Link).

### Mount, optics, heat and coordinates <a href="#mount-optics-heat-and-coordinates" id="mount-optics-heat-and-coordinates"></a>

Use the four bottom M3 holes, **6 mm depth**, allowing for bracket thickness. The quick start recommends a thermally conductive metal mounting surface and at least 10 mm surrounding clearance. Keep all sides ventilated and the optical field clear; an added transparent window can still alter performance. The body supports itself, not extra payload. Installation in another orientation requires matching external water protection; IP54 is not a waterproof cable or adapter rating. The portal offers a [protective-cover drawing](https://oss-global-cdn.unitree.com/static/f36779a1e788425b8f441a9df3b59936.zip). It describes an optional 5–6 g cover counterweight to reduce horizontal vibration while **increasing vertical vibration**; this is not a general instruction to rebalance a delivered unit. [Installation](https://support.unitree.com/home/en/L1_SDK/L1_Overview_install).

The point-cloud origin is the center of the bottom mounting plane: +X points opposite the cable outlet, +Y is 90° counterclockwise from +X, and +Z is normal to the base. IMU axes are parallel, but its origin must come from the exact L1 CAD; no L2 IMU offset is transferred. The reference converter includes a **0.0445 m Z bias** and calibration terms. Do not add that bias twice after using SDK output. Store the direction of each transform explicitly rather than merely writing “extrinsics calibrated.” [Preparation / coordinates](https://support.unitree.com/home/en/L1_SDK/L1_Overview_Use).

### State and LED behavior <a href="#state-and-led-behavior" id="state-and-led-behavior"></a>

Normal mode measures and streams. Standby is documented as <1 W with LED off and IMU-only output. **The preparation chapter says both motors stop; the viewer manual says the slower motor continues.** Treat standby as a commanded state, not proof that the assembly is safe to touch. An externally obstructed rotor can resume automatically when released. Remove power before clearing an obstruction.

L1's eight LED modes are command, forward slow/fast, reverse slow/fast, triple flip, triple breathing, and six-stage breathing. Command mode uses **45 bytes / 360 one-bit positions**; clear/invert/select-all/reset alter this display pattern. **Using the Unilidar Viewer automatically switches L1 to command LED mode, and the last LED mode is retained across power cycles.** Thus the Viewer observation workflow has a documented device-setting side effect even when no LED button is pressed; it is not configuration-neutral. This is an LED-mode change, not proof that the Viewer automatically changes every optical/acquisition setting. The documented power-on acquisition state is normal. Changing LEDs is a device write, not a read-only diagnostic. Viewer display accumulation, point size, opacity and reflectivity coloring affect presentation rather than the optical scan. [Preparation](https://support.unitree.com/home/en/L1_SDK/L1_Overview_Use), [viewer controls](https://support.unitree.com/home/en/L1_SDK/L1_unilidar).

## Software and development resources <a href="#software-and-development-resources" id="software-and-development-resources"></a>

### Exact SDK and prerequisites <a href="#exact-sdk-and-prerequisites" id="exact-sdk-and-prerequisites"></a>

The reviewed source is [unitreerobotics/unilidar\_sdk at `1bd7d95d8ab7ce7a22058d2bb07e39fd62612aa6`](https://github.com/unitreerobotics/unilidar_sdk/tree/1bd7d95d8ab7ce7a22058d2bb07e39fd62612aa6). It contains `unitree_lidar_sdk`, ROS and ROS2 workspaces. The portal's older separate-repository instructions are historical; use the actual pinned monorepo. The C++ interface uses the SDK's point type; PCL conversion is optional. Vendor reference environments are Ubuntu 20.04, ROS Noetic or ROS2 Foxy, and PCL 1.10 where applicable. These are source baselines, not proof of Humble/Jazzy, macOS, Windows-native C++ or arbitrary binary ABI support. Inspect the architecture-specific supplied library and license before integrating; source-visible wrappers do not mean all transport internals are open.

Adapt it with cancellation and `min(10, points.size())` before deliberate execution; do not run it as a harmless discovery script. Retain the exact configuration and delivered calibration instead of choosing whichever number yields an attractive cloud. ### MAVLink, calibrated points and timing <a href="#mavlink-calibrated-points-and-timing" id="mavlink-calibrated-points-and-timing"></a>

L1 serial raw data uses its **SysMavlink dialect**. Feed bytes through the appropriate MAVLink frame parser, validate complete frames, then decode message types; do not split a serial read into presumed packets. IMU payload contains quaternion\[4], angular\_velocity\[3], linear\_acceleration\[3] and a uint16 packet ID. The SDK example labels quaternion order **x,y,z,w**; downstream consumers must retain this ordering and verify acceleration/gyro scale for their exact interface.

Cloud reconstruction pairs distance and auxiliary messages by **equal `packet_id`**. Auxiliary data carries 120 reflectivities plus timing, motor periods, angular increments, temperature/voltage, optical quality, contamination, installation-angle errors and optical calibration. Distance data contains `point_data[240]`: 120 little-endian uint16 ranges; raw zero is invalid. `packet_cnt`, `payload_size` and actual available data must be checked before indexing. A corrupt, stale, missing or differently identified auxiliary frame must not be combined with a new distance line.

The published converter uses range scale 0.001, `b_axis_dist/1000`, `theta_angle`, `ksi_angle`, yaw/pitch progression, and a 0.0445 m base Z correction. This optical geometry is more than a naive spherical-to-Cartesian conversion. Preserve the calibrated model and actual units; portal snippets contain `Float` / `Return` capitalization mistakes and are explanatory, not compilable code to paste unchanged. The two motor periods in raw auxiliary data are microseconds, whereas the viewer also displays derived RPM. [Full development chapter / projection reference](https://support.unitree.com/home/en/L1_SDK/L1_develope).

Device time begins at startup; raw auxiliary fields separate seconds and microseconds. SDK cloud `stamp` and each relative point `time` must be interpreted with its host/device synchronization policy. Preserve packet IDs, discontinuities, restart boundaries and arrival times. A host wall-clock label or received timesync packet does not establish PTP, GNSS synchronization, or deterministic latency. The portal accidentally mentions “L2” in an L1 timing paragraph; this does not make the L1 and L2 protocols interchangeable.

### ROS, ROS2 and Windows bridge <a href="#ros-ros2-and-windows-bridge" id="ros-ros2-and-windows-bridge"></a>

ROS configuration covers port, yaw bias, range scale/bias/min/max, scan aggregation, cloud/IMU topics and frame names. Defaults `cloud_scan_num:18` and `range_max:50` are processing parameters, **not eighteen laser channels or a 50 m rated L1**. Topics are `unilidar/cloud`, `unilidar/imu`; frames are `unilidar_lidar`, `unilidar_imu`. Verify the measured transform before fusion or RViz interpretation. The actual ROS2 configuration lives in `unitree_lidar_ros2/src/unitree_lidar_ros2/launch/launch.py`; an old manual's ROS2 `catkin_make` instruction is inconsistent with the current ament/colcon package. [Pinned ROS2 launch](https://github.com/unitreerobotics/unilidar_sdk/blob/1bd7d95d8ab7ce7a22058d2bb07e39fd62612aa6/unitree_lidar_ros2/src/unitree_lidar_ros2/launch/launch.py).

The [Win64 chapter](https://support.unitree.com/home/en/L1_SDK/L1_win64sdk) documents `unilidar_publisher_udp.exe`, under the example installation `C:\Program Files\unitree_lidar_sdk 1.0.10\bin`. It opens the actual COM port and forwards parsed data to a chosen IP/port; `127.0.0.1` is a local-host destination, not a sensor factory IP. The subscriber consumes the publisher's output. Confirm serial ownership and protocol on both ends; don't run Viewer and publisher against the same COM port. This chapter does not prove that L1 natively supports Ethernet or SDK2 wire packets.

## Getting started <a href="#getting-started" id="getting-started"></a>

### First stationary cloud, conditional on the delivered kit <a href="#first-stationary-cloud-conditional-on-the-delivered-kit" id="first-stationary-cloud-conditional-on-the-delivered-kit"></a>

1. Identify L1 and its actual range variant. Inventory the matched 12 V adapter, L1 converter and USB data cable; do not substitute L2/SV1 harnesses. Fit a stable, ventilated mount with the rotor/field clear.
2. With supply disconnected, connect L1 to its converter and the matched power and USB paths. Then power the approved assembly. Keep people and loose cable clear of the rotating mechanism.
3. For the documented Windows 64-bit path, obtain [Unilidar from the official L1 downloads](https://www.unitree.com/download/LiDAR/), install its matching CP210x driver as directed, and select the **actual Silicon/CP210x COM port**, not a guessed COM number. Follow organizational policy for the vendor's administrator-execution instruction. **Connecting through Unilidar automatically selects command LED mode; the last LED mode can persist after power cycling. This Viewer check therefore includes that documented configuration change.**
4. Observe the stationary scene and read firmware/hardware/viewer identity, cleanliness and motor status. Start with a short 500 ms accumulation; 1/2/4 s increase display persistence. Rotate the view with the mouse/arrow keys; IMU-follow display changes view behavior. Avoid additional manual reset, parameter-save or LED commands merely to prove transport; this does not undo the Viewer's automatic LED-mode change.

**Expected result:** a current L1 cloud and status from the identified sensor, with IMU records where the actual acquisition state provides them. Save a screenshot and the version/configuration record; the viewer itself is not a dataset export tool. This is a documented future workflow, not a hardware test performed for this guide.

### Source-pinned development sequence <a href="#source-pinned-development-sequence" id="source-pinned-development-sequence"></a>

For an already supported host, inspect [the pinned SDK README](https://github.com/unitreerobotics/unilidar_sdk/blob/1bd7d95d8ab7ce7a22058d2bb07e39fd62612aa6/README.md), its CMake files, matched binary architecture, and the example's writes before building. A reproducible source reference is:

```bash
cd unilidar_sdk
git checkout 1bd7d95d8ab7ce7a22058d2bb07e39fd62612aa6
```

The standalone project is `unitree_lidar_sdk`; the ROS1 workspace uses `catkin_make`; the actual ROS2 workspace uses `colcon build`, sourcing its generated `install/setup.bash` before `ros2 launch unitree_lidar_ros2 launch.py`. These are separate paths, not commands executed during this review. Set the actual serial port, inspect serial-access permissions through the host's policy, and check cloud/IMU topics separately. Avoid global device permission changes. Expected SDK output is initialized reader/version information and parsed cloud/IMU records; a first ten-point print needs a length guard. A meaningful cancellation test must account for blocking `runParse()`, not merely add a loop clock.

### Original offline pair/length checkpoint <a href="#original-offline-pairlength-checkpoint" id="original-offline-pairlength-checkpoint"></a>

This self-contained Python example checks raw-line pairing and little-endian ranges with **synthetic input only**. It imports no Unitree software and opens no device. It deliberately does not claim to produce calibrated XYZ:

```python
def ranges_mm(aux_id, range_id, raw, reflectivity):
    if aux_id != range_id:
        raise ValueError("unmatched packet IDs")
    if len(raw) != 240 or len(reflectivity) != 120:
        raise ValueError("incomplete fixed-capacity line")
    return [(i, int.from_bytes(raw[2*i:2*i+2], "little"), reflectivity[i])
            for i in range(120) if raw[2*i:2*i+2] != b"\x00\x00"]

raw = (1000).to_bytes(2, "little") + bytes(238)
assert ranges_mm(7, 7, raw, bytes([42]) + bytes(119)) == [(0, 1000, 42)]
for args in [(7, 8, raw, bytes(120)), (7, 7, raw[:-1], bytes(120))]:
    try:
        ranges_mm(*args)
    except ValueError:
        pass
    else:
        raise AssertionError("unsafe line accepted")
print("synthetic pairing/length/zero-range checks passed")
```

This checks one boundary of a decoder. A real parser additionally needs MAVLink integrity/dialect checks, payload metadata, sequence/age handling and calibration. It is not a replacement for the manufacturer's complete parser.

## Troubleshooting and FAQs <a href="#troubleshooting-and-faqs" id="troubleshooting-and-faqs"></a>

| Observation                            | Next diagnostic and boundary                                                                                                                                                     |
| -------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| USB visible, no cloud                  | Verify the separate 12 V supply and L1 converter, actual COM selection, one port owner, acquisition state, and 2 Mbaud; a USB device is not a power-good indicator               |
| Cannot open serial / permission denied | Check actual device enumeration, another process holding the port, matching driver, and the host's approved serial-access group; relogin may be needed after a group change      |
| No cloud in standby                    | IMU-only output is documented; motor-stop descriptions conflict, so power off before touching                                                                                    |
| Point distortion / shifted axes        | Check range variant, exact yaw/calibration revision, scale, base bias, timestamps, assembly origin and measured extrinsics; don't tune calibration from an attractive screenshot |
| High dirt index / missing points       | Power down, gently clean optical window with clean cloth; small alcohol amount only as documented. Avoid scratches, glass covers and forced rotor contact                        |
| Rotor restarts unexpectedly            | Interference state can auto-resume after obstruction removal; standby is not electrical isolation                                                                                |
| Viewer cloud looks slow                | Accumulation time and rendering load are different from physical scan frequency; check transport/parse backlog and graphics capability                                           |
| UDP subscriber sees nothing            | Confirm the Windows publisher/optional converter, chosen host destination and port; do not assume L2 `.62:6101` endpoints                                                        |

[Official FAQ](https://support.unitree.com/home/en/L1_SDK/L1_FAQ). Store and transport dry and ventilated, isolated from corrosive/flammable materials; use retained protective packaging and secure the sensor against impact/friction. Periodically inspect long-stored equipment. Clean the window gently; do not dismantle optical calibration assemblies.

**Firmware and recovery:** public source history records compatibility examples and the viewer has restart/save controls, but an exact delivered-unit firmware image, signature/hash chain, rollback procedure, bootloader entry, and failed-update recovery are not established here. Seek the model/revision-matched manufacturer procedure. A restart is not a firmware restoration. The reviewed public static-library interface does not expose enough internals to guarantee passivity, timeout behavior, or every raw IMU scale.
